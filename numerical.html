OA.5 Numerical checks

Section 4 of the paper computes date-specific Markov policy functions by backward induction along the demographic transition, and selects among the candidate equilibria at each state; the technical documentation (Berg and Gonzalez-Eiras 2026) gives the algorithms and the code. This chapter reports two checks of that computation.

OA.5.1 Date-specific and stationary policies

The table prints the gap between the date-specific solution and a time-invariant policy function, the approximation of much of the quantitative politico-economic literature, at the same dates. Section 4 summarizes it in a footnote.

\(\cdot\)

One row per policy and IES, one column per date of the path. Argentina’s informal savers add a state, and a design chosen at \(t\) is weighed against the electorate of \(t+1\), so both differ from the stationary approximation even at an IES of one. The tax gaps are largest at the lowest IES, in Argentina above all, and every gap shrinks toward zero late in the horizon, apart from the last period shown.

OA.5.2 Equilibrium selection

The table prints, for every file of results behind an exhibit, the largest number of equilibria and of candidate solutions found at any state, and the number of states at which no candidate passed the equilibrium test and the solver fell back on the integral rule of the technical documentation. A single equilibrium at every state means that the selection rule of section 4 never had to choose. A file prints no count where its solve selects no tax, the economic-equilibrium paths, or where its driver did not record the count: the calibration grids of the U.S., the UK and France, the Frisch-elasticity and timing files, and the tax layer of the CRRA endogenous-design calibration and path files, whose design layer is counted in the lower panel. The paper’s statement in section 4 rests on the files that record the count.

Note: Per csv, the rows, the rows that record the counts, and over those the largest number of equilibria at any state and period, the largest number of candidates and the total number of states at which the solver fell back. The tax rule is the selection of the technical documentation, which falls back on the tax that maximizes its integral criterion where no candidate passes the equilibrium test; a solve that does not select a tax (the economic-equilibrium paths, with taxes held at the baseline path) or did not record its counts leaves them out. The design layer is that of the exact recursion under CRRA preferences, which compares the candidate designs with the retirees’ savings shares held fixed and then makes the shares consistent: its candidates are the brackets of that consistency residual, and where none closes to an equilibrium it falls back on comparing each design at its own consistent shares. escExperiments.csv and escExperimentsUK.csv, which the endogenous-design exhibits read, merge the escShocks* files, whose counts are the rows above.
