\subsection{Calibration}\label{\refsec:calibration}
The model is calibrated to Argentina, with 2010 as the baseline year. The inputs are in \texttt{data/ArgentinaTest.xlsx} and \texttt{data/argentina\_calibrationTargets.csv}, sourced in \texttt{data/README.md}. Parameters are constant over time except the ratio of workers to retirees $\nu_t$, and the $t$-subscript is dropped where a parameter is constant.

\smalltitle{Data.}
A period is thirty years, which suits the assumption that capital fully depreciates between periods. $\nu_t$ comes from population data and projections for 1980 to 2070, held at the 1980 value in 1920 and 1950 and at the 2070 value in 2100. A household survey gives the shares $\gamma$, hours and income of $J+1 = 5$ types, the informal type and four formal income quartiles, together with their propensities to vote $\mu_j$ and survival rates $p_j$, with $p_i = p$ common across the formal types.

The capital share is $\alpha = 0.35$ and the Frisch elasticity $\xi = 0.3$. The targets are a capital--output ratio of 3.23, Argentina's ratio in the Penn World Table in 2010, and a pension tax rate equal to pension spending, 7.1\% of GDP, over the labour share, $\tau_{2010} = 0.071/(1-\alpha) = 0.109$. Because a period is thirty years, the ratio the data report is thirty times the model's own $K_t/Y_t$ (\cref{\refnumsec:calibration}).

The ratio $RR$ of the replacement rates at mean and half-mean wages (OECD), represented by types $3$ and $1$, identifies $\theta$, which is exogenous in this model. The pre-reform universal pension $\epsilon$ is set from the pension rules rather than targeted: the basic pension was 70\% of the minimum pension, read as the benefit of the lowest formal type relative to the average formal benefit and discounted, with $\beta$, because it starts five (men) or ten (women) years after the formal retirement age. The reform that raised $\epsilon$ from 2010 is an experiment on the calibrated model, not a target.


\smalltitle{Calibration.}
The productivities $\eta_i$ and disutilities of labour $X_i$ of the formal types match income and hours. Let $z_j^{\eta}$ and $z_j^{x}$ denote the income and hours of type $j$ relative to the population-weighted mean over the \emph{formal} types, the same reference for every type, the informal one included, so that
\begin{align}\label{\refeq:calibration:z}
    \sum_{i}\gamma_i z_i^{\eta} = \sum_{i}\gamma_i z_i^{x} = 1,
\end{align}
where, as throughout, sums over $i$ run over the formal types only. Formal labour supply \eqref{\refeq:formalOpt} is $h_{t,i} = (\eta_i/X_i)^{\xi}\left[w_t(1-\tau_t)+\theta_{t+1}\overline{b}_{t+1}p_t/R_{t+1}\right]^{\xi}$, with the bracket common to all types, so that $h_{t,i}/h_t = (\eta_i/X_i)^{\xi}/\Gamma_h$ and the relative income and hours of the formal types are
\begin{align*}
        z_i^{\eta} &= \dfrac{w_t(1-\tau_t)h_{t,i} \eta_i}{w_t(1-\tau_t)h_t} = \dfrac{\eta_i^{1+\xi}/X_i^{\xi}}{\sum_{j} \gamma_ j\eta_j^{1+\xi}/X_j^{\xi}},  \\
        z_i^{x} &= \frac{h_{t,i}}{\sum_j\gamma_j h_{t,j}} = \frac{(\eta_i/X_i)^{\xi}}{\sum_j \gamma_j(\eta_j/X_j)^{\xi}}.
\end{align*}
Both are ratios, so the scales of $y_i^{\eta} \equiv \eta_i^{1+\xi}/X_i^{\xi}$ and $y_i^x \equiv (\eta_i/X_i)^{\xi}$ are free, and we fix them:
\begin{align}\label{\refeq:calibration:yNorm}
    \Gamma_h\equiv\sum_i\gamma_i\frac{\eta_i^{1+\xi}}{X_i^{\xi}} = 1, \qquad \sum_i\gamma_i\left(\frac{\eta_i}{X_i}\right)^{\xi} = 1.
\end{align}
The first sets the level of the labour aggregator. The second sets the unit of hours, which the rescaling $\eta_i\rightarrow\eta_i/\mu$, $X_i\rightarrow\mu^{-(1+\xi)/\xi}X_i$ changes without changing the equilibrium, and it matters for the informal calibration below: since $h_t/\sum_i\gamma_ih_{t,i} = \Gamma_h/\sum_i\gamma_i(\eta_i/X_i)^{\xi}$, only under \eqref{\refeq:calibration:yNorm} is $h_t$ average formal hours in the unit of $h_{t,i}$ and $h_{1,t}^0$, against which the informal hours target is stated. Then $\bm{y}^{\eta} = \bm{z}^{\eta}$ and $\bm{y}^{x} = \bm{z}^{x}$, and
\begin{align}
    \bm{\eta} &= \frac{\bm{y}^{\eta}}{\bm{y}^x} \label{\refeq:calibration:etai} \\
    \bm{X} &= \frac{\bm{\eta}}{(\bm{y}^x)^{1/\xi}}, \label{\refeq:calibration:Xi}
\end{align}
with bold symbols the vectors over the formal types and division and powers element by element.

For the informal type, \eqref{\refeq:calibration:z} and \eqref{\refeq:calibration:yNorm} give the relative income and hours of the young as
\begin{align*}
    z_0^{\eta} &= \frac{w_t^0h_{1,t}^0\eta_{0}}{w_t(1-\tau_t)h_t}, \\
    z_0^x &= \dfrac{h_{1,t}^0}{\sum_i \gamma_{i} h_{t,i}} = \frac{h_{1,t}^0}{h_t}.
\end{align*}
Write $k_t\equiv s_{t-1}/\nu_t$. In equilibrium $h_t = \Theta_{h,t}k_t^{\alpha\xi/(1+\alpha\xi)}$, so the factor prices \eqref{\refeq:factorPrices} and \eqref{\refeq:w0} give $w_t = (1-\alpha)\Theta_{h,t}^{-\alpha}k_t^{\alpha/(1+\alpha\xi)}$ and $w_t^0 = \left((1-\alpha)/\Gamma_{h,t}^{\alpha}\right)^{1/(1+\alpha\xi)}k_t^{\alpha/(1+\alpha\xi)}$. The ratio $z_0^{\eta}/z_0^x = \eta_0w_t^0/\left(w_t(1-\tau_t)\right)$ then identifies $\eta_0$, and the informal labour supply condition \eqref{\refeq:informalOpt}, $h_{1,t}^0 = (\eta_0w_t^0/X_0)^{\xi} = z_0^x\Theta_{h,t}k_t^{\alpha\xi/(1+\alpha\xi)}$, identifies $X_0$; $k_t$ drops out of both:
\begin{align}
    \eta_{0} &= \frac{z_0^{\eta}}{z_0^x}\frac{(1-\alpha)(1-\tau_t)}{\Theta_{h,t}^{\alpha}\left(\frac{1-\alpha}{\Gamma_{h,t}^{\alpha}}\right)^{\frac{1}{1+\alpha\xi}}} \label{\refeq:calibration:eta0}\\
    X_{0} &= \eta_{0}\frac{\left(\frac{1-\alpha}{\Gamma_{h,t}^{\alpha}}\right)^{\frac{1}{1+\alpha\xi}}}{\left(\Theta_{h,t}z_0^x\right)^{1/\xi}}, \label{\refeq:calibration:X0}
\end{align}
evaluated in 2010. The coefficient $\Theta_{h,t}$ depends on the politico-economic equilibrium, so the two conditions are targets solved together with it.

Given the formal $\eta_i$ and $X_i$, $\theta$ (constant over time) follows from the ratio of replacement rates,
 \begin{align*}
        RR \equiv \dfrac{\theta+(1-\theta)\frac{h_t}{\eta_3 h_{t,3}}}{\theta+(1-\theta)\frac{h_t}{\eta_1 h_{t,1}}} = \frac{\theta + (1-\theta)\frac{\Gamma_{h,t} X_3^{\xi}}{\eta_3^{1+\xi}}}{\theta+(1-\theta)\frac{\Gamma_{h,t} X_1^{\xi}}{\eta_1^{1+\xi}}},
\end{align*}
where types $1$ and $3$ stand for half-mean and mean wages, so that
\begin{align}
    \theta = \frac{RR \frac{\Gamma_{h,t} X_1^{\xi}}{\eta_1^{1+\xi}} - \frac{\Gamma_{h,t} X_3^{\xi}}{\eta_3^{1+\xi}}}{1-\frac{\Gamma_{h,t} X_3^{\xi}}{\eta_3^{1+\xi}}-RR\left(1-\frac{\Gamma_{h,t} X_1^{\xi}}{\eta_1^{1+\xi}}\right)}.
\end{align}

The remaining four parameters need the politico-economic equilibrium path: the political weight $\omega$ and the discount factor $\beta$, with $\beta_{t,j} = \beta p_j$, set so that the equilibrium reproduces the capital--output ratio and the tax rate of 2010, and $\eta_0, X_0$, which satisfy \eqref{\refeq:calibration:eta0} and \eqref{\refeq:calibration:X0} at the equilibrium $\Theta_{h,2010}$. Since the equilibrium cannot be solved without them, the four are found jointly as a nested fixed point (\cref{\refnumsec:calibration}).
