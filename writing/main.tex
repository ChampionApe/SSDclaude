\begin{abstract}
\noindent
This note documents the models and the data behind the paper \textit{Social Security Design and Its Political Support}, in several places in more generality than the paper uses. It has four parts: the informal analytical model, the informal savings model, the U.S.\ model, and the data of the paper's OECD cross-section, which sets out how the pension design and the tax rate of each country are measured. Each model part first characterizes the economic equilibrium, in which taxes and pension characteristics are given, and the politico-economic equilibrium, and then sets out how the model is solved numerically. Each part is self-contained, so some text recurs across parts.

Households differ in productivity and in $X$, the disutility of hours. The informal analytical model calibrates a type-specific $X$; the informal savings and U.S.\ models are calibrated either with one $X$ common to all household types or with type-specific values. In every model the tax rate is chosen each period by a government that cannot commit to future policy. The pension design $\theta$, and in the two informal models the universal pension $\epsilon$, are exogenous, except in the U.S.\ model, where the electorate can also choose $\theta$: once and for all at a reform date, or period by period, one period ahead, without commitment.
\end{abstract}
