\subsection{The economic equilibrium}\label{\refsec:EE}

\smalltitle{With log preferences.}
Given the paths of taxes $\tau_t$ and designs $\theta_t$ and an initial level of savings $s_0$, and with $B_{t+1}^i = \beta_{t,i}$, the economic equilibrium needs no numerical root finding:
\begin{itemize}
    \item compute $\Gamma_{s,t}$ from \eqref{\refeq:auxiliary:Gammas} for $t<T$;
    \item compute $\Theta_{h,t}$ from \eqref{\refeq:auxiliary:Thetah} for $t<T$ and \eqref{\refeq:auxiliary:ThetahT} for $t=T$, and $\Theta_{s,t}$ from \eqref{\refeq:auxiliary:Thetas} for $t<T$;
    \item iterate forward on \eqref{\refeq:auxiliary:s} from $s_0$ for $t<T$, with $s_T = 0$.
\end{itemize}
These core variables give individual labour supply and savings, factor prices, pension benefits and, from the budgets, consumption (\cref{\refmodelsec}).


\smalltitle{With CRRA preferences.}
With the same inputs, the core of the economic equilibrium is the vectors $\Gamma_{s,t}, h_t, s_t$, identified by a square system of nonlinear equations: the labour supply condition \eqref{\refeq:auxiliary:h}, with $\Theta_{h,t}$ from \eqref{\refeq:auxiliary:Thetah} for $t<T$ and \eqref{\refeq:auxiliary:ThetahT} for $t=T$; the savings condition \eqref{\refeq:auxiliary:sFromH} for $t<T$, with $s_T = 0$; and \eqref{\refeq:auxiliary:Gammas} for $t<T$, evaluated with $B_{t+1}^i(s_t, h_{t+1})$ from \eqref{\refeq:auxiliary:B}. The rest of the outcomes follow as in the log case.


\smalltitle{Initializing with steady state savings.}
By default the initial level of savings $s_0$ is the steady state at the parameters and policy of the first period. This default is also what makes the scale invariance of \eqref{\refmodeleq:scaleInvariance} operative: $s_0$ is then a function of the same parameters as everything else and rescales with them.

With log preferences $B^i = \beta_i$, so $\Gamma_{s}$ is the closed form \eqref{\refeq:auxiliary:Gammas} and steady state savings follow from \eqref{\refeq:steadystate_LOG:s}:
\begin{subequations}\label{\refeq:steadystate_LOG}
    \begin{align}
        \Gamma_s =& \frac{1}{1+\xi}\frac{\sum_i \frac{\gamma_{i}\eta_{i}^{1+\xi}}{X_{i}^{\xi}}\frac{B^i}{1+B^i}}{1+\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\tau\left(\theta+(1-\theta)\sum_i\frac{\gamma_{i}}{1+B^i}\right)} \label{\refeq:steadystate_LOG:Gammas}\\
         s^* =& \Gamma_h^{1+\xi}\left[\left(\frac{(1-\alpha)(1-\tau)}{\Gamma_h-\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\theta\tau\Gamma_s}\right)^{1+\xi}\frac{\Gamma_s^{1+\alpha\xi}}{\nu^{\alpha(1+\xi)}}\right]^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha}} \label{\refeq:steadystate_LOG:s}
    \end{align}
\end{subequations}

With CRRA preferences, we have to identify $s^*$ numerically instead, as a bounded scalar root problem in $\Gamma_s$ solved by Brent's method: we solve for the $\Gamma_s$ that satisfies condition \eqref{\refeq:steadystate_CRRA:Gammas},
\begin{subequations}\label{\refeq:steadystate_CRRA}
\begin{align}
    &\Gamma_s(1+\xi)\left[1+\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\tau\left(\theta+(1-\theta)\sum_i\frac{\gamma_i}{1+B^i(\Gamma_s)}\right)\right] = \sum_i\frac{\gamma_i\eta_i^{1+\xi}}{X_i^{\xi}} \frac{B^i(\Gamma_s)}{1+B^i(\Gamma_s)}, \label{\refeq:steadystate_CRRA:Gammas} \\
    &\text{where }B^i(\Gamma_s) = \left(\beta_i\right)^{\rho} \left(\frac{\alpha}{p}\frac{\Gamma_h-\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\theta\tau\Gamma_s}{(1-\alpha)(1-\tau)}\frac{\nu}{\Gamma_s}   \right)^{\rho-1} \label{\refeq:steadystate_CRRA:Bi}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
Two bounds on $\Gamma_s$ matter, and only the first is the obvious one. The right hand side of \eqref{\refeq:steadystate_CRRA:Gammas} is at most $\Gamma_h$ and the bracket on the left is at least one, so with constant $B^i$ across types
\begin{align}\label{\refeq:steadystate_CRRA:bounds}
    \Gamma_s \in \left(0, \frac{\Gamma_h}{1+\xi}\frac{B}{1+B}\right).
\end{align}
This is a bound on where the root \emph{is}. The second is a bound on where the residual can be \emph{evaluated at all}. Both $\Theta_{h,t}$ in \eqref{\refeq:auxiliary:Thetah} and $B^i$ in \eqref{\refeq:steadystate_CRRA:Bi} carry the factor $\Gamma_h-\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\theta\tau\Gamma_s$, which vanishes at
\begin{align}\label{\refeq:steadystate_CRRA:cap}
    \Gamma_s^{\max}\left(\tau,\theta\right) = \frac{\Gamma_h\,\alpha}{(1-\alpha)\,\theta\tau},
\end{align}
and is negative above it. A steady state always satisfies $\Gamma_s<\Gamma_s^{\max}$, since $\Theta_{h}$ must be real and positive, so \eqref{\refeq:steadystate_CRRA:cap} never excludes the root. What it does exclude is a part of the search interval: raising $\Gamma_s$ past $\Gamma_s^{\max}$ raises a negative number to the fractional power $\rho-1$, and the residual returns NaN rather than a finite value of the wrong sign, on which a bracketing method fails.

The bracket is therefore $\left(l_{\Gamma},\min\left\lbrace u_{\Gamma}, \varsigma\,\Gamma_s^{\max}(\tau,\theta)\right\rbrace\right)$, with $l_{\Gamma} = 10^{-6}$, $u_{\Gamma} = 0.75$ and a safety factor $\varsigma = 0.99$, and where it does not bracket a sign change its upper end is doubled, up to $\varsigma\,\Gamma_s^{\max}$, until it does. A constant upper end fails in both directions: it is too high where the cap falls below it, at large $\theta\tau$, and at $\theta = 0$, where $\Gamma_s^{\max}$ is infinite, the root can lie above it (at $\rho = 2$ and $\tau$ near one). Both regions are visited, since the steady state is evaluated at trial taxes spanning $[l,u]$ while the politico-economic equilibrium is solved. $\Gamma_h$ is kept explicit in \eqref{\refeq:steadystate_CRRA:bounds} and \eqref{\refeq:steadystate_CRRA:cap}: the US calibrations normalise it to one, but the France and UK calibrations under variant A end at $\Gamma_h\neq1$ (\cref{\refmodelsec:calibration:targets}).
