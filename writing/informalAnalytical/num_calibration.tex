\subsection{Calibration}\label{\refsec:calibration}
The calibration is a nested fixed point. In the inner part, we solve for the full politico-economic equilibrium path; in the outer part, we search over the parameters that cannot be set without it. The target system is repeated from \cref{\refmodelsec:calibration}:
\begin{subequations}\label{\refeq:calibration}
    \begin{align}
        \widehat{K/Y}_{t_0} &= n_{\text{yr}}\frac{s_{t_0-1}/\nu_{t_0}}{\left(s_{t_0-1}/\nu_{t_0}\right)^{\alpha} h_{t_0}^{1-\alpha}} = n_{\text{yr}}\frac{\alpha}{R_{t_0}} \label{\refeq:calibration:KY}\\
        \hat{\tau}_{t_0} &= \tau_{t_0} \label{\refeq:calibration:tau}\\
        \hat{\eta}_{0} &= \frac{z_0^{\eta}}{z_0^x}\frac{(1-\alpha)(1-\tau_{t_0})}{\Theta_{h,t_0}^{\alpha}\left(\frac{1-\alpha}{\Gamma_{h,t_0}^{\alpha}}\right)^{\frac{1}{1+\alpha\xi}}} \label{\refeq:calibration:eta0}\\
        \hat{X}_{0} &= \hat{\eta}_{0}\frac{\left(\frac{1-\alpha}{\Gamma_{h,t_0}^{\alpha}}\right)^{\frac{1}{1+\alpha\xi}}}{\left(\Theta_{h,t_0}z_0^x\right)^{1/\xi}} \label{\refeq:calibration:X0}
    \end{align}
\end{subequations}
where the ``hats'' reflect exogenous targets and $t_0$ indicates a specific baseline year. In our main specification, we assume that $\beta_{t,j} = \beta$ is constant over time and across types, and that most exogenous parameters do not change over time except for the ageing population reflected by $\nu_t$. The four parameters searched over are then assigned to the four targets as follows: $\beta$ carries the capital--output ratio, $\omega$ the tax rate, and $\eta_0, X_0$ the informal income and labour targets --- an assignment that describes the dominant terms of the Jacobian, not a licence to solve the equations one at a time.

\smalltitle{Why the capital--output ratio.}
The first target \eqref{\refeq:calibration:KY} carries the factor $n_{\text{yr}}=30$, the number of years in a model period, and that factor is the whole content of the target: a period's output $Y_{t_0}$ is thirty years of production while $s_{t_0-1}/\nu_{t_0}$ is a stock, so the model's own ratio $K_{t_0}/Y_{t_0}$ is a \emph{thirtieth} of the capital--output ratio that national accounts report. The savings rate
\begin{align}
    \text{sr}_{t_0} &= \frac{s_{t_0}}{\left(s_{t_0-1}/\nu_{t_0}\right)^{\alpha} h_{t_0}^{1-\alpha}} \label{\refeq:calibration:sr}
\end{align}
is reported throughout but is not targeted, because it is not the annual saving rate the national accounts measure: with capital fully depreciating between periods, $s_{t_0}$ \emph{is} the next period's capital stock, so \eqref{\refeq:calibration:sr} is $K_{t_0+1}/Y_{t_0}$ --- a stock over a period's flow. Targeting the capital--output ratio avoids the conversion entirely: it is measured directly and depends on the predetermined stock $s_{t_0-1}$ alone.

\smalltitle{The procedure.}
In an inner loop, given a trial parameter vector, we first refresh all auxiliary parameter definitions that depend on it (so that derived parameters like $\kappa$ are consistent with the trial values), and then re-solve the entire politico-economic equilibrium path by the methods of \cref{\refsec:PEELOG} or \cref{\refsec:PEE}. An outer loop searches over $\left(\beta, \omega, \eta_0, X_0\right)$ --- each mapped to the real line by a logarithm, so that positivity is enforced without a bound being reached --- until \eqref{\refeq:calibration} holds. Note that $\beta$ is a genuinely global object in this scheme: it moves $s_{t_0-1}$ through every period preceding the baseline year as well as $h_{t_0}$ within it, which is why the inner solve cannot be shortened to the baseline period.
