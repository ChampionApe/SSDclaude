\section{Further results on the endogenous design}\label{app:escResults}\setcounter{equation}{0}
This appendix collects results on the endogenous design of section \ref{sec:esc} that the main text only points to: the calibration of the cost for the UK at each intertemporal elasticity, and the path of the design along the projected U.S.\ demography.

\subsection{Calibration}\label{app:escResults:calibration}
Table \ref{table:UK_ESC:calibration} calibrates the cost on the UK as table \ref{table:US_ESC:calibration} does on the U.S.: $\lambda$ is the value at which the UK electorate re-elects its observed design, $\theta^{\ast} = 0.560$, with households grouped at the UK's own income cuts as in appendix \ref{app:US}, and at $\rho = 1$ it is the UK's own parameter of section \ref{sec:esc:deepTest}. The UK's parameter lies below the U.S.\ one at low elasticities, by $33\%$ at $\rho = 0.5$ and $32\%$ at $\rho = 1$, and above it at $\rho = 2$, by $17\%$. The cost weighs less in the UK. At $\rho = 1$ its observed design loses $1.9\%$ of revenue, as the U.S.\ one does, but a flat benefit of the same size would lose $9.2\%$, against $22.9\%$ in the U.S., because the UK's dispersion of relative incomes is about half the U.S.\ one, $0.223$ against $0.436$, and its cost parameter a third lower, although its system is slightly larger, with a tax rate of $11.2\%$ against $10.5\%$. As in the U.S., the cost raises the calibrated political weight of the old, from $1.10$ to $1.15$ at $\rho = 1$.

\subsection{The design along the baseline path}\label{app:escResults:path}
Figure \ref{fig:US_ESC:path} follows the U.S.\ along its projected demography at $\rho = 1$, with the design chosen and with the design pinned at $\theta^{\ast}$. Since the calibration targets only the design of 2020, the path from 2020 on is a prediction. Along it the chosen design goes from $0.728$ in 2020 to $0.802$ in 2050 and stays at $0.820$ from 2110 on. It tracks the number of workers per retiree, which falls from $1.34$ to $1.07$, and settles one period after the demography does. The tax rate climbs from $10.5\%$ to $16.9\%$, $0.3$ p.p.\ above the path with the design pinned, since a more earnings-related system loses less of its revenue.\footnote{The path barely depends on the Frisch elasticity. Recalibrated at $\xi = 0.2$ and $0.4$, $\lambda$ changes by less than one percent and the tax rate in 2110 by about $0.3$ p.p., while the design in 2110 and the design under acute ageing move by about $0.001$ (\oa{esc-parameters}).}
