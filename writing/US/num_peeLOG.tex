\subsection{The politico-economic equilibrium with log}\label{\refsec:PEELOG}
Throughout, let $z_t$ denote the marginal effect of policy on the political objective,
\begin{align}\label{\refeq:LOG:foc}
    z_t &= \omega\sum_{i}\left(\gamma_{t-1,i}p_{t-1}\mu_{t-1,i} \frac{d\upsilon_{2,t}^i}{d\tau_t}\right)+\nu_t\sum_i\left(\gamma_{t,i}\mu_{t,i} \frac{d\upsilon_{1,t}^i}{d\tau_t}\right),
\end{align}
where the marginal effects on indirect utilities are defined by \eqref{\refmodeleq:PEELOG} for $t<T$ and \eqref{\refmodeleq:terminalPEELOG} for $t=T$.

\smalltitle{The problem separates into $T$ scalar problems.}
As established in \eqref{\refmodeleq:PEELOG:decoupling}, $z_t$ is a function of $\tau_t$ alone. Three consequences follow. First, there is no recursion: the periods may be solved in any order, or all at once. Second, the $T$-dimensional root problem $\bm{z}(\bm{\tau}) = \bm{0}$ has a \emph{diagonal} Jacobian, so a vectorized Newton step is $T$ independent scalar Newton steps sharing one function evaluation, and there is no cross-period information for a solver to exploit. Third, robustness is local: a period in which the first order condition is badly behaved cannot contaminate any other period. Everything in this subsection could therefore be stated for a single, arbitrary $t$; the vector notation reflects that the implementation evaluates all periods in one pass.

\smalltitle{Behaviour at the boundaries.}
Both $d\upsilon_{1,t}^i/d\tau_t$ and $\partial \ln(\Theta_{h,t})/\partial\tau_t$ carry a factor $1/(1-\tau_t)$, so $z_t\rightarrow -\infty$ as $\tau_t\rightarrow 1$. We therefore always evaluate on $[l,u]$ with $u<1$ strictly. The divergence makes it easy to choose $u$ close enough to $1$ that $z_t(u)<0$, and we assume this throughout. Two consequences follow: the upper corner is then never selected, and $z_t(l)>0$ guarantees by continuity that at least one interior downward crossing exists. The substantive difficulty is thus not existence, but (i) detecting a lower-corner solution and (ii) choosing between several interior crossings when they occur, the difficulties \cref{\refsec:RobustRoot} is built for. Both are verified numerically rather than assumed, since $z_t(u)<0$ is a property of the calibration, not an identity.

\begin{alg}[Gradient-based roots for the politico-economic equilibrium with log preferences]
\label{\refalg:LOG:fast}
Let $\tilde{\bm{\tau}} \in \mathbb{R}^{T}$ be a candidate vector of policies and $\bm{\tau}$ the bounded version where each $\tau_t \in [l, u]$. Solve \eqref{\refeq:root} for every period at once with a gradient-based root finder, with $z_t$ from \eqref{\refeq:LOG:foc}, and report $\bm{\tau}$ as the bounded version. The bounds are $l = 0$ and $u = 1-10^{-4}$, the upper end of the grid: with $u = 1$ the bounded tax of the first period could reach one, where the steady state that supplies the savings shares of the first period's retirees (\cref{\refsec:EE}) is undefined. The algorithm has no fallback and presumes that the first order condition is well behaved.
\end{alg}

\begin{alg}[Grid search over the full path]\label{\refalg:LOG:gridsearch}
Fix $\bm{\theta}$ and a grid $\mathcal{G}_{\tau}$ on $[l,u]$. For each $t$, independently of every other period:

\smallskip\noindent\emph{Evaluate.} Form $z_t$ on $\mathcal{G}_{\tau}$, using \eqref{\refmodeleq:terminalPEELOG} at $t=T$ and \eqref{\refmodeleq:PEELOG} elsewhere. Every entry is a closed-form expression of $\tau_t$ and the period's parameter vintage.

\smallskip\noindent\emph{Select.} Construct the candidate set $\mathcal{K}_t$ of \eqref{\refeq:candidates} (the two corners alone when $z_t$ does not change sign on $\mathcal{G}_{\tau}$), apply the equilibrium test \eqref{\refeq:equilibriumTest} at each candidate's own savings shares, and select among the equilibria as in \cref{\refsec:RobustRoot}, recording their number.

\smallskip
The cost is $O(T\cdot M)$ evaluations of closed-form expressions, with no derivatives and no initial guess. Because the policy path is smooth in $t$, the evaluation may be restricted to a window of nodes around the neighbouring period's solution, widened whenever the selection sits on its edge; the window is a convenience, and widening it cannot change the answer in any other period.
\end{alg}

\smalltitle{The solve as run.}
The two algorithms are combined. \Cref{\refalg:LOG:fast} runs first, from a constant path at the observed tax or from the last solution. \Cref{\refalg:LOG:gridsearch} then runs on the full grid at every solve, which locates the selected equilibrium and reports the candidate and equilibrium counts. If the gradient solution lies within one grid cell of the selected equilibrium it is kept; otherwise the grid selection is polished by \cref{\refalg:LOG:fast} started from it, so that the grid locates the branch and the gradient step attains solver precision. Should the polish fail, the grid solution is returned and flagged.
