B1. Calibration

The calibrations across the intertemporal elasticity

Each country is calibrated at every ρ of the grid against its own targets. The discount factor is calibrated on the U.S. and imposed on the UK and France, so their interest factors are predictions. The political weight of the old falls as the IES rises in every country, since the young resist taxation less, and it keeps its ranking: highest in the U.S., close behind in France, lowest in the UK. The savings rate falls with the IES in every country, and the UK’s and France’s interest factors rise toward the U.S. target. The taste for leisure, pinned by each country’s average workweek, falls a little with the IES in the UK and France and is nearly flat in the U.S. At the lowest elasticities of the grid the thirty-year discount factor exceeds one, which the UK and France inherit.
