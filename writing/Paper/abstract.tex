\begin{abstract}
\noindent


The design of social security systems shapes the political support for intergenerational transfers. In an overlapping generations model with heterogeneous households and probabilistic voting, we study how contributive vs.\ universal pensions and the link between benefits and earnings affect the social security tax rate, labor supply and capital accumulation. Taxes are lower when benefits are earnings-related, and a universal pension raises them when informal retirees are poorer than formal ones. Calibrated to Argentina, the model accounts for about three fifths of the rise in pension spending over the 2005--2010 expansion of coverage. Calibrated to the U.S., it makes ageing to a constant population the main determinant of pension spending and the earnings link the second. When the electorate also chooses the design, an interior choice requires redistribution through pensions to be costly unless the rich vote much more often than the poor; with such a cost, ageing ties benefits closer to earnings. Across 26 OECD countries, the model has pension spending and the earnings link rise together as population growth falls, as in the data, while whether the earnings link follows inequality depends on the propensities to vote.
