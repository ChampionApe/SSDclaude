\subsection{Endogenous system characteristics}\label{\refsec:ESC}
Everything up to this point treats the pension design $\theta_t$ as a parameter, imposed from the design index or identified from the ratio of replacement rates (\cref{\refsec:calibration:theta}). This section makes it a political outcome. Two ingredients are needed, and they are logically independent: a \emph{cost} that keeps the choice off the boundary, and a \emph{timing} convention that says when the design is chosen and what it is expected to bind for.

Without a cost the choice is degenerate. Under the sequential timing below the design only re-splits a fixed pot among retirees whose marginal utilities differ, and where propensities to vote do not rise with income the objective falls in $\theta_t$ on the whole unit interval; under the leaded and permanent timings (\cref{\refsec:ESC:timing}) the costless choice at the US calibration under log preferences is the same Beveridgean corner, $\theta = 0$ (\cref{\refnumsec:ESC:checks}).


\smalltitle{The deadweight cost of redistributive benefits.}
The flat component of a pay-as-you-go benefit taxes some contributors and subsidises others, and we make it costly for that reason, in a reduced form that leaves the structure of the model intact. Write the benefit formula as
\begin{align}\label{\refeq:esc:benefits}
    b_t^i = \left[A(\theta_t,\tau_t)h_{t-1,i}\eta_{t-1,i} + B(\theta_t,\tau_t)h_{t-1}\right]\overline{b}_t,
\end{align}
with $\overline{b}_t$ still given by \eqref{\refeq:governmentBudget:bbar}. The baseline model is $\left(A,B\right) = \left(\theta,1-\theta\right)$, where the two coefficients sum to one and \eqref{\refeq:esc:benefits} reduces to the formula in \eqref{\refeq:governmentBudget}. With a cost they sum to less than one, and the form of the shortfall is derived rather than assumed.

\emph{The implicit tax.} Let $y_{t,i}\equiv y_{t,i}^{\eta}/\Gamma_{h,t}$ denote the labour income of type $i$ relative to the mean (the $z_i^{\eta}$ of \eqref{\refeq:calibration:zeta} at vintage $t$), so that $\eta_{t,i}h_{t,i}/h_t = y_{t,i}$ by \eqref{\refeq:EE:hi} and $\sum_i\gamma_{t,i}y_{t,i}=1$. A retiree of type $i$ at $t$ draws $\left[\theta_t y_{t-1,i}+1-\theta_t\right]h_{t-1}\overline{b}_t$ on past earnings $w_{t-1}y_{t-1,i}h_{t-1}$, in expectation $\Pi_t\tau_t\left[\theta_t + (1-\theta_t)/y_{t-1,i}\right]$ per unit of those earnings, where $\Pi_t\equiv p_{t-1}\overline{b}_t/(\tau_tw_{t-1}) = \nu_tw_th_t/(w_{t-1}h_{t-1})$ by \eqref{\refeq:governmentBudget:bbar} is the pay-as-you-go return factor: the benefit per unit of earnings that a purely earnings-related system pays out of the same revenue. The flat component moves type $i$ away from that benchmark by
\begin{align}\label{\refeq:esc:implicitTax}
    t_{t,i} \equiv \Pi_t\,\tau_t\,(1-\theta_t)\left(1-\frac{1}{y_{t-1,i}}\right)
\end{align}
per unit of earnings: a tax on types above mean income, a subsidy to types below it, and a pure transfer in the aggregate, since $\sum_i\gamma_{t-1,i}y_{t-1,i}t_{t,i}=0$. It is the redistributive part of the tax component of the contribution, the part of the payroll charge that buys the contributor no benefit.

\emph{Which margin.} The wedge \eqref{\refeq:esc:implicitTax} is an \emph{average} implicit tax. The marginal wedge of the flat component, $\Pi_t\tau_t(1-\theta_t)$ per unit of earnings, is common to all types and already in the model, through the group $\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}A\tau_{t+1}\Gamma_{s,t}$ in $\Theta_{h,t}$, and GHH preferences carry no income effect through which the transfers between types could move hours. A cost on the hours margin would count that response twice. The cost therefore stands for margins the model does not contain, participation and formality, on which the average wedge is the relevant one; the paper's appendix on the endogenous design reviews the evidence on these margins.

\emph{The Harberger loss.} A set of proportional wedges $t_i$ on bases $e_i$, each base responding to its net-of-tax rate with a common elasticity $\varepsilon$, costs $\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon\sum_i\gamma_it_i^2e_i$ to second order in the wedges. The wedge \eqref{\refeq:esc:implicitTax} falls on the benefit paid at $t$ per unit of earnings made at $t-1$, which the contributor values at $1/R_t$ as in the labour supply condition \eqref{\refeq:formalOpt}, so the rate that enters is $t_{t,i}/R_t$. Per unit of mean past earnings the bases are $e_i = y_{t-1,i}$, so the loss is $\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon\,(\Pi_t/R_t)^2\tau_t^2(1-\theta_t)^2\tilde{V}_{t-1}$ with
\begin{align}\label{\refeq:esc:Vtilde}
    \tilde{V}_{t} \equiv \sum_i\gamma_{t,i}\frac{\left(y_{t,i}-1\right)^2}{y_{t,i}},
\end{align}
while revenue per unit of mean past earnings is worth $(\Pi_t/R_t)\tau_t$. As a share of revenue the loss is therefore $\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon\,(\Pi_t/R_t)\,\tau_t\,(1-\theta_t)^2\,\tilde{V}_{t-1}$: quadratic in the share of the system that is flat, linear in its size, and proportional to a dispersion of relative income that is zero when all incomes are equal and, being a function of $y^{\eta}/\Gamma_h$ alone, invariant to the normalisations of \eqref{\refeq:scaleInvariance} and \eqref{\refeq:hoursUnit}. The quadratic counts the subsidy to types below the mean as a loss, as it does the tax on types above it, a symmetry of the reduced form rather than of the argument.

\emph{Specification.} We adopt the exponential form
\begin{align}\label{\refeq:esc:AB}
    f(\theta_t,\tau_t) &\equiv \exp\left(-\tfrac{1}{2}\,\lambda\,\tau_t\,\tilde{V}_{t-1}\,(1-\theta_t)^2\right), &
    \left(A,B\right) &= \left(f(\theta_t,\tau_t)\,\theta_t,\; f(\theta_t,\tau_t)\,(1-\theta_t)\right),
\end{align}
so that the whole system carries the cost, the surviving share is $A+B=f\in(0,1]$, and $1-f$ is the share of revenue that does not arrive; to first order it is the Harberger share, with $\lambda$ in the place of $\varepsilon\Pi_t/R_t$.\footnote{The factor $\Pi_t/R_t$ multiplies every implicit tax alike and so scales the loss once. It moves with demographics, and the cost of the paper absorbs it into a constant $\lambda$, calibrated at the baseline date rather than derived. The $\lambda$ of this section is the cost parameter of the paper and is unrelated to the rescaling factor of \eqref{\refeq:scaleInvariance}.} A variant keeps the factor (\texttt{sizePiR} in the code): $\lambda$ is replaced by $\varepsilon\pi_t$, with $\pi_t = \Pi_t/R_t$ at the date of the benefits that $f$ scales, which is how the paper carries the cost across countries. Households and voters take $\pi_t$ as given, as an aggregate of the path, so $\partial_{\tau}\ln f$ does not see it, and every solve under the variant iterates $\pi_t$ to its value on the equilibrium path being solved (the solvers of the permanent timing refuse it).

The exponential keeps $f$ positive for every $\lambda$ and every size of system, nests the baseline at $\lambda=0$, and is \emph{proportional}: the cost scales both components alike and cancels from every ratio of benefits. Nothing is lost when nothing is redistributed: $f=1$ at $\theta_t=1$, and for every design when $\tilde{V}_{t-1}=0$. The loss per unit of redistribution rises with the size of the system, through $\tau_t$. And the marginal cost of the first unit of redistribution is zero: $\partial_{\theta}\ln f = \lambda\tau_t\tilde{V}_{t-1}(1-\theta_t)$ and $\partial_{\tau}\ln f$ vanish at $\theta_t=1$, so under log preferences the slope of the political objective at the Bismarckian corner does not depend on $\lambda$, and the cost cannot by itself make that corner a local maximum. The government's budget is unchanged in form: it collects $\nu_t w_t \tau_t h_t$ and delivers $\left(A+B\right)h_{t-1}\overline{b}_tp_{t-1}$ per member of the retiring cohort, so $\overline{b}_t$ is \emph{gross} revenue per unit of past labour and $1-f$ the share that does not arrive.

\emph{Dates.} $f$ takes the design and the tax of the date whose benefits it scales: $(\theta_t,\tau_t)$ in \eqref{\refeq:esc:benefits} and in the consumption of current retirees, and $(\theta_{t+1},\tau_{t+1})$ wherever the young anticipate their own benefits, with $\tau_{t+1} = \mathcal{T}_{t+1}(\theta_{t+1})$ the continuation tax. $\tilde{V}$ carries the vintage of the cohort whose benefits it prices, $\tilde{V}_{t-1}$ in the benefit formula and $\tilde{V}_t$ in the terms of the young.

\emph{Magnitude.} The cost is attached to the transfer the design performs rather than to the design: a wedge $f(\theta) = \phi+(1-\phi)\theta^{p}$ on both components, the comparison specification of the paper's online appendix, burns a share $1-\phi$ of revenue at $\theta = 0$ even when all incomes are equal. The shape of \eqref{\refeq:esc:AB} follows from the tax component and its size does not: the calibrated $\lambda$ lies well above what participation and taxable-income elasticities imply (the paper gives the comparison), and it stands in as well for administrative, evasion and political costs of redistribution that the model does not contain.


\smalltitle{What the cost changes, and what it does not.}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:esc:substitution}
In every equation of the economic equilibrium, the design enters only through the two groups $\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\tau_{t+1}\theta_{t+1}$ and $\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\tau_{t+1}(1-\theta_{t+1})$ (and their date-$t$ counterparts in the benefit formula). The cost is therefore \emph{exactly} the substitution $\theta_{t+1} \mapsto A(\theta_{t+1},\tau_{t+1})$ and $(1-\theta_{t+1})\mapsto B(\theta_{t+1},\tau_{t+1})$ in those groups, and $\theta_t \mapsto A(\theta_t,\tau_t)$, $(1-\theta_t)\mapsto B(\theta_t,\tau_t)$ in the benefit formula, and changes nothing else in the economic equilibrium. In the political equilibrium it adds one factor: because $A$ and $B$ depend on the tax that funds them, the term of the retired in the tax first order condition picks up $\partial_{\tau}\ln f$.
\end{proposition}

The claim can be read off \eqref{\refnumeq:auxiliary}: $\theta_{t+1}$ appears in $\Gamma_{s,t}$, $\Theta_{h,t}$ and $s_{t,i}/s_t$ and nowhere else, and always in one of the two groups. Applying the substitution,
\begin{subequations}\label{\refeq:esc:auxiliary}
\begin{align}
    \Gamma_{s,t} &= \frac{1}{1+\xi}\frac{\sum_i \gamma_{t,i}y_{t,i}^{\eta}\frac{B_{t+1}^i}{1+B_{t+1}^i}}{1+\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\tau_{t+1}\left(A(\theta_{t+1},\tau_{t+1})+B(\theta_{t+1},\tau_{t+1})\sum_i\frac{\gamma_{t,i}}{1+B_{t+1}^i}\right)},
    \label{\refeq:esc:auxiliary:Gammas} \\
    \Theta_{h,t} &= \left(\Gamma_{h,t}\right)^{\frac{1+\xi}{1+\alpha\xi}}\left(\frac{(1-\alpha)(1-\tau_t)}{\Gamma_{h,t}-\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}A(\theta_{t+1},\tau_{t+1})\tau_{t+1}\Gamma_{s,t}}\right)^{\frac{\xi}{1+\alpha\xi}},
    \label{\refeq:esc:auxiliary:Thetah}
\end{align}
and, in the log case where $B_{t+1}^i = \beta$ is common,
\begin{align}
    \frac{s_{t,i}}{s_t} &= \frac{y_{t,i}^{\eta}}{\Gamma_{h,t}} + \frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\frac{B(\theta_{t+1},\tau_{t+1})\tau_{t+1}}{1+\beta}\left(\frac{y_{t,i}^{\eta}}{\Gamma_{h,t}}-1\right).
    \label{\refeq:esc:auxiliary:si}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
Second-period consumption picks up the substitution at date $t$ rather than $t+1$: the bracket $1+\theta_t\left(y_{t-1,i}-1\right)$ appearing in \eqref{\refeq:EE:ci} becomes $A(\theta_t,\tau_t)\,y_{t-1,i} + B(\theta_t,\tau_t)$. The two coincide when $A+B=1$, so the baseline is nested exactly and not merely as a limit. The one place where the cost does more than substitute is the term of the retired in the tax first order condition, \eqref{\refeq:PEELOG:v2i} and its CRRA counterpart in \eqref{\refeq:PEE}: the bracket there is multiplied by $\tau_t$, and $f$ now moves with $\tau_t$, so
\begin{align}\label{\refeq:esc:dlnc2i}
    \frac{d\ln\left(c_{2,t}^i\right)}{d\tau_t} = (1-\alpha)\frac{d\ln\left(h_t\right)}{d\tau_t} + \frac{\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\left[A(\theta_t,\tau_t)\,y_{t-1,i}+B(\theta_t,\tau_t)\right]\left(1+\tau_t\,\partial_{\tau}\ln f(\theta_t,\tau_t)\right)}{\frac{s_{t-1,i}}{s_{t-1}}+\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\tau_t\left[A(\theta_t,\tau_t)\,y_{t-1,i}+B(\theta_t,\tau_t)\right]},
\end{align}
where $\partial_{\tau}\ln f(\theta_t,\tau_t) = -\tfrac{1}{2}\lambda\tilde{V}_{t-1}(1-\theta_t)^2$, so that $1+\tau_t\partial_{\tau}\ln f = 1+\ln f(\theta_t,\tau_t)$ because $\ln f$ is linear in $\tau_t$: a larger system is worth less to a retiree at the margin than the pot it collects. Nothing else in the tax first order condition sees $f$ under log preferences, since \eqref{\refeq:PEELOG:v1i} and \eqref{\refeq:PEELOG:dThetah} are free of the groups; under CRRA the numerical derivatives of the young's utilities see $f(\theta_{t+1},\tau_{t+1})$ through the continuation only.

A consequence of \cref{prop:esc:substitution}: the cost does \emph{not} disturb the decoupling result of \eqref{\refeq:PEELOG:decoupling}. The first order condition for $\tau_t$ involves $\theta_{t+1}$ only through the level of $\Theta_{h,t}$, which reaches it only through the old informal household, absent here, and the new factor in \eqref{\refeq:esc:dlnc2i} is dated $t$, so $z_t$ remains a function of $\left(\tau_t,\theta_t\right)$ alone.


\smalltitle{Identification of $\theta$.}
The datum survives the cost: under \eqref{\refeq:esc:benefits} a retiree's benefit per unit of mean past earnings, $\Pi_t\tau_t\left[A\,y_{t-1,i}+B\right]$, is a line in relative earnings whose earnings-linked share at mean earnings, the design index of \cref{\refsec:calibration:theta}, is $A/(A+B) = \theta_t$. On the route without that datum, the ratio of replacement rates pins
\begin{align}\label{\refeq:esc:RR}
    \frac{B(\theta,\tau)}{A(\theta,\tau)} = \frac{1-RR_0}{RR_0/z_1^{\eta} - 1/z_2^{\eta}},
\end{align}
with $j=1,2$ the half-mean and mean-income groups of \eqref{\refeq:calibration:zeta}, and $f$ cancels again. Either way $\theta$ is the number the exogenous-design model uses, whatever the cost parameter, and the design and the cost are identified separately. This is a property of the specification: a cost on the flat component alone, $(A,B) = (\theta,\, f\,(1-\theta))$, gives the share $\theta/(\theta+f(1-\theta))$ and the ratio $(1-\theta)f/\theta$, so the same datum implies a different $\theta$ for every value of the cost parameter.


\subsubsection{Three timings}\label{\refsec:ESC:timing}
Let $\mathcal{W}_t$ denote the political objective of \cref{\refsec:PEELOG}, evaluated with the same weights that determine $\tau_t$. We consider three conventions for when the design is chosen. Throughout, $s_{t-1,i}/s_{t-1}$ is \emph{predetermined} at $t$: the electorate takes the inherited distribution of savings as given, exactly as it does when choosing $\tau_t$.

\smalltitle{Sequential.} $\theta_t$ is chosen at $t$ alongside $\tau_t$, and binds for that period only. Because benefits at $t$ are paid on labour supplied at $t-1$, and because current labour supply responds to $\theta_{t+1}$ rather than $\theta_t$, the design has no effect on any date-$t$ quantity other than the split of a fixed pot among retirees. Writing $A'$ and $B'$ for the partial derivatives of $A(\theta_t,\tau_t)$ and $B(\theta_t,\tau_t)$ with respect to $\theta_t$ at the given $\tau_t$, the marginal effect under log preferences is
\begin{align}\label{\refeq:esc:seqFOC}
    \dfrac{\partial \mathcal{W}_t}{\partial \theta_t} = \omega\sum_i\gamma_{t-1,i}p_{t-1}\mu_{t-1,i}\,
    \frac{\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\tau_t\left[A'(\theta_t,\tau_t)\frac{y_{t-1,i}^{\eta}}{\Gamma_{h,t-1}} + B'(\theta_t,\tau_t)\right]}
         {\frac{s_{t-1,i}}{s_{t-1}}+\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\tau_t\left[A(\theta_t,\tau_t)\frac{y_{t-1,i}^{\eta}}{\Gamma_{h,t-1}}+B(\theta_t,\tau_t)\right]},
\end{align}
and under CRRA preferences each summand is multiplied by $\left(c_{2,t}^i\right)^{1-1/\rho}$. From \eqref{\refeq:esc:AB}, $A' = f + \theta_t f_{\theta}$ and $B' = (1-\theta_t)f_{\theta} - f$, where $f_{\theta} = f\,\lambda\tau_t\tilde{V}_{t-1}(1-\theta_t)$. Without a cost, $A' = 1$ and $B' = -1$, and the numerator is proportional to $y_{t-1,i}^{\eta}/\Gamma_{h,t-1}-1$, which averages to zero across types under the population weights alone, while the denominator is increasing in the same object: lowering $\theta_t$ moves resources from retirees with low marginal utility to retirees with high marginal utility. The voting weights $\mu_{t-1,i}$ rise with income in the US calibration, so there the sign is established numerically: on the solved baseline path the sum is negative for every $\theta_t\in[0,1]$ at every date, under log preferences and at $\rho = 0.5$ and $2$ (\cref{\refnumsec:ESC:checks}), and the choice is the corner $\theta_t=0$. With a cost, $A'+B' = f_{\theta}\geq 0$, positive away from $\theta_t=1$ and zero there, and the sign is no longer determined by the distribution alone.

\smalltitle{Leaded.} $\theta_{t+1}$ is chosen at $t$ and becomes a state at $t+1$. The design now reaches current labour supply, through $A(\theta_{t+1},\tau_{t+1})$ and $B(\theta_{t+1},\tau_{t+1})$ in \eqref{\refeq:esc:auxiliary}, and through it every date-$t$ quantity, but it no longer touches the benefits of current retirees:
\begin{align}\label{\refeq:esc:leadFOC}
    \dfrac{d\upsilon_{2,t}^i}{d\theta_{t+1}} &= (1-\alpha)\dfrac{d\ln\left(\Theta_{h,t}\right)}{d\theta_{t+1}}, &
    \dfrac{d\upsilon_{1,t}^i}{d\theta_{t+1}} &= (1+\beta_{t,i})\dfrac{d\ln\left(\tilde{c}_{1,t}^i\right)}{d\theta_{t+1}} + \beta_{t,i}\dfrac{d\ln\left(R_{t+1}\right)}{d\theta_{t+1}},
\end{align}
where each total derivative includes the response of the continuation policy $\tau_{t+1}$, and of $\theta_{t+2}$, to the design being chosen, the former now also through the cost, since $f$ depends on $\tau_{t+1}$. Retirees are unanimous here, since the first expression does not depend on $i$, and at a given continuation tax they favour a more contributive system: a higher $\theta_{t+1}$ raises the return to an hour worked at $t$ and hence $h_t$, on which their benefits are levied. The young are divided, and the sign of the aggregate is a quantitative matter.

\smalltitle{Permanent.} At a reform date $t_0$ the electorate chooses a design expected to hold forever, $\theta_t = \theta$ for all $t\geq t_0$. Before $t_0$ the design is exogenous and constant at its observed value, and the vote is \emph{anticipated}: households arrive at $t_0$ knowing a design will be chosen, which determines the value of the predetermined savings ratio the vote is taken against (\cref{\refnumsec:ESC:permanent}). All three channels operate at once: the sequential re-split of \eqref{\refeq:esc:seqFOC}, the labour-supply channel of \eqref{\refeq:esc:leadFOC}, and the continuation, which now moves the entire future path of taxes. The marginal effects add,
\begin{align}\label{\refeq:esc:permFOC}
    \dfrac{d\upsilon_{2,t_0}^i}{d\theta} &= (1-\alpha)\dfrac{d\ln\left(\Theta_{h,t_0}\right)}{d\theta} + \mathcal{E}_{2,t_0}^{i,\theta}, &
    \dfrac{d\upsilon_{1,t_0}^i}{d\theta} &= (1+\beta_{t_0,i})\dfrac{d\ln\left(\tilde{c}_{1,t_0}^i\right)}{d\theta} + \beta_{t_0,i}\dfrac{d\ln\left(R_{t_0+1}\right)}{d\theta},
\end{align}
with $\mathcal{E}_{2,t_0}^{i,\theta}$ the fraction in \eqref{\refeq:esc:seqFOC} at $t_0$, i.e.\ its summand without the weight $\omega\gamma_{t_0-1,i}p_{t_0-1}\mu_{t_0-1,i}$. The electorate chooses $\tau_{t_0}$ and $\theta$ together, which appears to make the problem two-dimensional. It does not:

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:esc:concentration}
Under the permanent timing, the optimal tax at any candidate design is the ordinary politico-economic equilibrium tax at that design, $\tau^{\ast}(\theta) = \mathcal{T}_{t_0}(\theta)$, and the joint problem concentrates to a one-dimensional maximisation over $\theta$.
\end{proposition}

The argument: $\partial\mathcal{W}_{t_0}/\partial\tau_{t_0} = 0$, taken with $s_{t_0-1,i}/s_{t_0-1}$ held fixed, \emph{is} the first order condition $z_{t_0}=0$ evaluated at $\theta_{t_0}=\theta$, including the factor $\left(1+\tau_{t_0}\partial_{\tau}\ln f\right)$ of \eqref{\refeq:esc:dlnc2i}, which $z_{t_0}$ carries. The permanent timing adds nothing to it, because $\theta$ is not a function of $\tau$. Likewise, for $t>t_0$ the design is fixed and the continuation is the ordinary equilibrium of \cref{\refsec:PEELOG} at a constant $\theta$, so there is no recursion in the design to solve.


\subsubsection{Two structural properties of the log case}\label{\refsec:ESC:separability}
The log specification removes state variables from the design problem altogether.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:esc:separability}
Under log preferences, the date-$t$ political objective is additively separable,
\begin{align}\label{\refeq:esc:separability}
    \mathcal{W}_t = \mathcal{A}\left(\tau_t,\theta_t\right) + \mathcal{B}\left(\theta_{t+1}\right) + \mathcal{C}\left(s_{t-1}\right),
\end{align}
where $\mathcal{C}$ depends on no policy. Consequently the leaded choice of $\theta_{t+1}$ depends on neither $s_{t-1}$ nor $\theta_t$: the maximiser of \eqref{\refeq:esc:separability} is the maximiser of $\mathcal{B}$ alone.
\end{proposition}

To see \eqref{\refeq:esc:separability}, note that $\Theta_{h,t}$ in \eqref{\refeq:esc:auxiliary:Thetah} is a product of a factor in $\tau_t$ and a factor in $\left(\tau_{t+1},\theta_{t+1}\right)$, with $\tau_{t+1} = \mathcal{T}_{t+1}(\theta_{t+1})$ by decoupling, so $\ln h_t$ is a sum of a term in $\tau_t$, a term in $\theta_{t+1}$ and a multiple of $\ln(s_{t-1}/\nu_t)$. The same holds for $\ln s_t$, which adds $\ln\Gamma_{s,t}$, and for $\ln \tilde c_{1,t}^i$, whose bracket involves $\left(\theta_{t+1},\tau_{t+1}\right)$ only. The return $\ln R_{t+1}$ is affine in $\ln s_t$ and $\ln\Theta_{h,t+1}$, which depends on $\tau_{t+1}$ and on the continuation $\left(\theta_{t+2},\tau_{t+2}\right)$; by backward induction from the terminal period, where no design is chosen, the design chosen at $t+1$ depends on no state, so the continuation is constant. Finally $\ln c_{2,t}^i$ is $(1-\alpha)\ln h_t + \alpha\ln(s_{t-1}/\nu_t)$ plus a constant and the log of $s_{t-1,i}/s_{t-1} + \frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\tau_t\left[A(\theta_t,\tau_t)y_{t-1,i} + B(\theta_t,\tau_t)\right]$, a function of $\left(\tau_t,\theta_t\right)$, since $s_{t-1,i}/s_{t-1}$ is by \eqref{\refeq:esc:auxiliary:si} at $t-1$. Every term of $\mathcal{W}_t$ is therefore a function of $\left(\tau_t,\theta_t\right)$, of $\theta_{t+1}$ or of $s_{t-1}$.

The inherited $\theta_t$ is a state for the tax, through $z_t\left(\tau_t,\theta_t\right)$, but not for the design: the electorate's preferred $\theta_{t+1}$ is the same whatever system it inherits. The property is specific to log preferences (under CRRA the powers do not separate, and both $s_{t-1}$ and $\theta_t$ re-enter), and it is checked numerically rather than relied upon (\cref{\refnumsec:ESC:checks}).

\Cref{prop:esc:separability} does not extend to the permanent timing, where $\theta$ appears in $\mathcal{A}$ and $\mathcal{B}$ at once. There the predetermined ratio $s_{t_0-1,i}/s_{t_0-1}$ is a function of the design in force at $t_0$, yet the savings behind it are sunk when the vote is taken, so holding it fixed while varying the candidate is the definition of the problem, not an approximation; and because the vote is anticipated, it is held at the value the winning design implies, which makes the permanent equilibrium a fixed point (\cref{\refnumsec:ESC:permanent}).
