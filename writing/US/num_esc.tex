\subsection{Endogenous system characteristics}\label{\refsec:ESC}
This subsection documents how the design choices of \cref{\refmodelsec:ESC} are solved. The sequential choice needs no solver: its sign is established by evaluating \eqref{\refmodeleq:esc:seqFOC} without the cost on the solved baseline path, over a grid of $\theta_t$ at every date, with each summand weighted by $(c_{2,t}^i)^{1-1/\rho}$ under CRRA preferences. Throughout, $\mathcal{G}_{\theta}$ denotes a grid of inherited designs on $[0,1]$, $\mathcal{G}_{\theta}^{\prime}$ a grid of candidate designs, $\mathcal{T}_t(\theta)$ the equilibrium tax at date $t$ given the design in force (the solution of $z_t(\tau,\theta)=0$ selected as in \cref{\refsec:RobustRoot}), and $\Theta_t(\cdot)$ the design chosen at $t$ as a function of the state. Under log preferences $\mathcal{T}_t$ is, by decoupling, a static scalar problem per design.

\smalltitle{Why a grid and not a first order condition.} The question being asked is whether the choice is interior, and a solver built on $\mathrm{d}\mathcal{W}/\mathrm{d}\theta = 0$ answers it only by failing to converge. We therefore evaluate the objective itself on a grid of designs, take the maximiser, and refine it by a polynomial through the nodes around it when the maximum is interior (a cubic through four nodes in \cref{\refalg:esc:leaded}, a parabola through three elsewhere); a corner is returned \emph{as} a corner. The discipline on predetermined states of \cref{\refsec:RobustRoot} carries over: every candidate design is evaluated at one inherited distribution of savings. Under log preferences and the leaded timing this is automatic, since that distribution depends on $(\tau_t,\theta_t)$ alone; under CRRA preferences (\cref{\refsec:ESC:crra}) and the permanent timing (\cref{\refsec:ESC:permanent}) it has to be imposed.


\subsubsection{The leaded choice}\label{\refsec:ESC:leaded}
The design chosen at $t$ is a state at $t+1$, so the objects to identify are the sequences of policy functions $\mathcal{T}_t(\theta_t)$ and $\Theta_t(\theta_t)$. \Cref{prop:esc:separability} says the second is constant in its argument; the algorithm tabulates it on $\mathcal{G}_{\theta}$ so that the property is checked rather than used.

\begin{alg}\label{\refalg:esc:leaded}
\emph{Leaded choice, log preferences.} Let $\mathcal{G}_{\theta}^{\prime}$ be finer than $\mathcal{G}_{\theta}$.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \emph{Terminal period.} Solve $\mathcal{T}_{T}(\theta)$ for every $\theta\in\mathcal{G}_{\theta}\cup\mathcal{G}_{\theta}^{\prime}$ from the terminal first order condition \eqref{\refmodeleq:terminalPEELOG}; there is no $\theta_{T+1}$ to choose. Then iterate backwards over $t = T-1,\dots,1$.
    \item \emph{Taxes.} Evaluate $z_t$ on the flattened mesh $\left(\mathcal{G}_{\theta}\cup\mathcal{G}_{\theta}^{\prime}\right)\times\mathcal{G}_{\tau}$ in a single call ($z_t$ is elementwise in both arguments, so the mesh costs one evaluation rather than one per design) and select at each node as in \cref{\refsec:RobustRoot}. \emph{Polish} each interior selection by a bracketed scalar root find within one grid cell. The taxes on $\mathcal{G}_{\theta}$ are the policy $\mathcal{T}_t$; those on $\mathcal{G}_{\theta}^{\prime}$ are the anticipated tax that the choice at $t-1$ reads at its candidates.
    \item \emph{Design.} For each $\theta_t\in\mathcal{G}_{\theta}$, evaluate
    \begin{align}\label{\refeq:esc:leadedObjective}
        \mathcal{W}_t\left(\theta';\theta_t\right) = \sum_i \gamma_{t-1,i}\omega p_{t-1}\mu_{t-1,i}\ln\left(c_{2,t}^i\right) + \nu_t\sum_i \gamma_{t,i}\mu_{t,i}\left[(1+\beta_{t,i})\ln\left(\tilde{c}_{1,t}^i\right)+\beta_{t,i}\ln\left(R_{t+1}\right)\right]
    \end{align}
    at every candidate $\theta'\in\mathcal{G}_{\theta}^{\prime}$, using $\tau_t = \mathcal{T}_t(\theta_t)$ from step 2 and the continuation from the periods already solved: $\tau_{t+1} = \mathcal{T}_{t+1}(\theta')$, solved at $\theta^{\prime}$ in step 2 of period $t+1$, $\theta_{t+2} = \Theta_{t+1}(\theta')$ and $\tau_{t+2} = \mathcal{T}_{t+2}(\theta_{t+2})$. Set $\Theta_t(\theta_t)$ to the maximiser over $\mathcal{G}_{\theta}^{\prime}$, refined when interior by the cubic through the maximising node, its two neighbours and the next node on the side of the higher neighbour.
\end{enumerate}
\end{alg}

Three implementation points. \emph{The polish is not optional}: the grid selection alone is accurate to $O(\text{spacing}^2)$, while $\tau_{t_0}$ is a calibration target matched at $10^{-8}$; corners are never polished. \emph{$s_{t-1}$ is set to one}, since by \cref{prop:esc:separability} the maximiser does not depend on it. \emph{The anticipated tax is solved, not interpolated}: the choice reads $\mathcal{T}_{t+1}$ at candidate designs between the nodes of $\mathcal{G}_{\theta}$, and on an objective this flat at its maximum the secant slope of a piecewise-linear table moves the argmax, which, unlike a root, has no residual through which the error would show. The tax at a given design being a static scalar problem, step 2 solves it at every candidate. The policies read between the nodes of $\mathcal{G}_{\theta}$ remain piecewise linear and do not move the argmax: $\Theta_{t+1}$ is constant by \cref{prop:esc:separability}, and $\mathcal{T}_{t+2}$ is read at $\Theta_{t+1}(\theta^{\prime})$, the same design at every candidate.


\subsubsection{The permanent choice}\label{\refsec:ESC:permanent}
Here the design is chosen once, at $t_0$, and the electorate chooses $\tau_{t_0}$ alongside it. \Cref{prop:esc:concentration} reduces what looks like a two-dimensional grid search over $\left(\tau_{t_0},\theta\right)$ to a one-dimensional one. The permanent timing is solved in the regression suites only; no result of the paper uses it.

\begin{alg}\label{\refalg:esc:permanent}
\emph{Permanent choice.} Given a pinned ratio $\bar{\sigma}_i$, for each $\theta\in\mathcal{G}_{\theta}^{\prime}$: (i) obtain $\tau_t = \mathcal{T}_t(\theta)$ for every $t\geq t_0$, for $t>t_0$ the ordinary equilibrium at a constant design; (ii) evaluate \eqref{\refeq:esc:leadedObjective} at $t_0$ with $\theta_{t_0}=\theta'=\theta$ and $s_{t_0-1,i}/s_{t_0-1}=\bar{\sigma}_i$. Take the refined maximiser. The vote being anticipated, $\bar{\sigma}_i$ is itself the ratio implied by the winning design, so the design is the fixed point of
\begin{align}\label{\refeq:esc:permFixedPoint}
    \theta^{\ast} = \arg\max_{\theta\in\mathcal{G}_{\theta}^{\prime}}\ \mathcal{W}_{t_0}\left(\theta;\ \sigma_i(\theta^{\ast})\right),
\end{align}
reached by iterating the above from the incumbent design. Convergence is reported, not assumed: this is a best response and not a contraction, so a cycle has to be visible.
\end{alg}

\smalltitle{The predetermined ratio is held fixed, at the winning design's value.} Under the permanent timing $\theta$ appears in $s_{t_0-1,i}/s_{t_0-1}$, through $\theta_{t_0}$. But savings made at $t_0-1$ are sunk when the vote is taken, so recomputing the ratio at every candidate would credit the electorate with moving a state it takes as given, and would be inconsistent with $\mathcal{T}_{t_0}$, which solves $z_{t_0}=0$ at a fixed ratio. The reform being anticipated, those savings were made against the design that wins, which is \eqref{\refeq:esc:permFixedPoint}; the incumbent's ratio, the pinning for a surprise reform, seeds the iteration, and the two coincide where the chosen design reproduces the incumbent one, the calibration condition \eqref{\refeq:esc:calibration}. The fixed point is scalar because the ratio \eqref{\refmodeleq:esc:auxiliary:si} at vintage $t_0-1$ depends on date-$t_0$ policy alone, so the kinked path of the reform, incumbent before $t_0$ and chosen from $t_0$, delivers the same $\bar{\sigma}_i$ as a design that had always been $\theta$.


\subsubsection{CRRA preferences}\label{\refsec:ESC:crra}
The powers in $\left(\tilde{c}\right)^{1-1/\rho}$ do not turn products into sums, so \eqref{\refmodeleq:esc:separability} fails, both $s_{t-1}$ and $\theta_t$ re-enter the design choice, and the Markov object is a policy function over the two-dimensional state $\left(s_{t-1},\theta_t\right)$. For the permanent timing each candidate is one ordinary CRRA equilibrium solve at a constant $\theta$ path, and \cref{\refalg:esc:permanent} goes through unchanged. For the leaded timing the backward recursion of \cref{\refsec:PEE} is extended with the design as a second state (\cref{\refalg:esc:crra2D}); a path iteration that approximates it is kept as a cross-check (\cref{\refalg:esc:crraPath}).

\smalltitle{The equilibrium at a state.} At $t$ the electorate inherits $\left(s_{t-1},\theta_t\right)$ and the distribution of past savings $D_t\equiv\left\lbrace s_{t-1,i}/s_{t-1}\right\rbrace_i$, and chooses $\left(\tau_t,\theta_{t+1}\right)$. $D_t$ is \eqref{\refmodeleq:EE:si_s} at vintage $t-1$: a function of $\left(\tau_t,\theta_t\right)$ and of $\bm{B}_t$, which under CRRA carries $R_t$, hence $s_{t-1}$ and current hours. With a discount factor common to all types, which every calibration imposes and the solver asserts, it is the one-parameter family
\begin{align}\label{\refeq:esc:aDef}
    \frac{s_{t-1,i}}{s_{t-1}} = 1 + a_t\left(y_{t-1,i}-1\right), \qquad a_t = 1+\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\,\tau_t\,\frac{f\left(\theta_t,\tau_t\right)\left(1-\theta_t\right)}{1+B_t},
\end{align}
with $y_{t-1,i}$ the relative income of \cref{\refmodelsec:ESC}, $B_t = B_t\left(s_{t-1},h_t\right)$ the common value of $B_t^i$, and $f$ the cost of \eqref{\refmodeleq:esc:AB}; the scalar $a_t$ is all the state the retirees' term carries. Under log preferences $B_t=\beta$ and $a_t$ is a function of $\left(\tau_t,\theta_t\right)$ alone; under CRRA it moves with hours, and hours with the candidate design.

The electorate takes $\left(s_{t-1},D_t\right)$ as given: both are savings decisions of the previous generation, whose anticipation of the policy chosen at $t$ pins their equilibrium value through consistency without making either a choice at $t$. \Cref{\refalg:esc:crra2D} therefore values every candidate design, and the tax it would be paired with, at one $a_t$, and closes $a_t$ afterwards by consistency. A policy $\left(\hat{\tau},\hat{\theta}^{\prime}\right)$ and a scalar $a$ form an equilibrium at the state if
\begin{subequations}\label{\refeq:esc:stateEq}
\begin{align}
    \hat{\tau} &= \arg\max_{\tau\in[l,u]}\ \mathcal{W}_t\left(\tau,\hat{\theta}^{\prime};\,a\right), \label{\refeq:esc:stateEq:tax}\\
    \hat{\theta}^{\prime} &= \arg\max_{\theta^{\prime}\in[0,1]}\ \mathcal{V}_t\left(\theta^{\prime};a\right), \qquad \mathcal{V}_t\left(\theta^{\prime};a\right)\equiv\max_{\tau\in[l,u]}\ \mathcal{W}_t\left(\tau,\theta^{\prime};\,a\right), \label{\refeq:esc:stateEq:design}\\
    a &= a_t\left(\hat{\tau},\,h_t\left(\hat{\tau},\hat{\theta}^{\prime}\right);\,\theta_t\right), \label{\refeq:esc:stateEq:consistency}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
where $\mathcal{W}_t\left(\tau,\theta^{\prime};a\right)$ is the CRRA form of \eqref{\refeq:esc:leadedObjective}, with $\upsilon_{1,t}^i = \left(\hat{c}_{1,t}^i\right)^{1-1/\rho}/(1-1/\rho)$ of \eqref{\refeq:hatc1i} and $\upsilon_{2,t}^i = \left(c_{2,t}^i\right)^{1-1/\rho}/(1-1/\rho)$, evaluated at the date-$t$ equilibrium at $\left(\tau,\theta^{\prime}\right)$ with $c_{2,t}^i$ formed from the frozen $a$. In \eqref{\refeq:esc:stateEq:design} the tax is re-optimised at every candidate design at the \emph{same} $a$, as an electorate that chose another design would set its tax against the savings it inherited. Condition \eqref{\refeq:esc:stateEq:tax} at \eqref{\refeq:esc:stateEq:consistency} is the equilibrium test \eqref{\refeq:equilibriumTest} of the tax rule; \eqref{\refeq:esc:stateEq} adds that the design is tested at the same frozen state, a scalar fixed point at every state, which under log preferences collapses to \cref{\refalg:esc:leaded}. Valuing each candidate design at its own consistent distribution instead would add to the derivative of the objective in $\theta^{\prime}$ the term $\partial_a\mathcal{W}_t\cdot\mathrm{d}a_t/\mathrm{d}\theta^{\prime}$, a gain from moving last period's savings that the electorate cannot obtain; it runs through $\mathrm{d}\ln B_t/\mathrm{d}\theta^{\prime} = (\rho-1)(1-\alpha)\,\mathrm{d}\ln h_t/\mathrm{d}\theta^{\prime}$ and the response of the tax, and vanishes as $\rho\rightarrow1$.

\begin{alg}\label{\refalg:esc:crra2D}
\emph{Leaded choice, CRRA preferences.} Let $\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{G}_{\theta}$ be a grid over the state $\left(s_{t-1},\theta_t\right)$ and $\mathcal{G}_{\theta}^{\prime}$ a grid of candidate designs. Solve the terminal period as in \cref{\refsec:PEE} once per $\theta_T\in\mathcal{G}_{\theta}$; it has no design to choose. Then, backwards over $t$:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \emph{States.} For every $\left(\tau_t, s_{t-1}, \theta^{\prime}\right) \in \mathcal{G}_{\tau}\times\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{G}_{\theta}^{\prime}$, resolve the equilibrium state $s_t$ from the state residual \eqref{\refeq:stateResidual}, with the continuation $\left(\tau_{t+1}, h_{t+1}\right)$ read off the period-$(t+1)$ policy interpolants of step 5 at $\left(s_t, \theta^{\prime}\right)$; then $h_t$, $B_t$, and the young's terms of $z_t$ and of $\mathcal{W}_t$, including the numerical $\tau$-derivatives of \cref{\refalg:CRRA:grid}. None of these involves $\theta_t$ or $a$: the inherited design and distribution enter the retirees' term alone, which is closed form in $\left(\tau_t,h_t;\theta_t,a\right)$ and is the only term rebuilt below.
    \item \emph{The range of the state.} At each $\left(s_{t-1},\theta_t\right)\in\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{G}_{\theta}$, evaluate the consistent $a_t$ of \eqref{\refeq:esc:aDef} at every $\left(\tau_t,\theta^{\prime}\right)$ of the policy grid, and let $\mathcal{G}_{a}$ be a grid of $M_a$ nodes ($M_a = 5$) spanning its range, extended by one cell at each end. A fixed point of \eqref{\refeq:esc:stateEq} lies in that range, since the consistent $a_t$ at any policy does.
    \item \emph{Best responses at a frozen state.} For each $a\in\mathcal{G}_{a}$ and each candidate $\theta^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}_{\theta}^{\prime}$, assemble $z_t\left(\cdot\,,\theta^{\prime};a\right)$ along $\mathcal{G}_{\tau}$ with the retirees' shares frozen at $a$, and take $\hat{\tau}\left(\theta^{\prime};a\right)$ as the maximiser over $\mathcal{G}_{\tau}$ of its integral, the profile \eqref{\refeq:objectiveProfile} with $D_c$ replaced by $a$; with $a$ frozen the integral is the objective up to a constant, and no equilibrium test is needed. An interior maximiser is located at the crossing of $\hat{z}_t$ in a cell adjacent to the maximising node (the nearer one if both), and $\mathcal{V}_t\left(\theta^{\prime};a\right)$ is $\mathcal{W}_t$ there, read by the quadratic through the three nearest nodes. Then $\hat{\theta}^{\prime}(a)$ is the maximiser of $\mathcal{V}_t\left(\cdot\,;a\right)$ over $\mathcal{G}_{\theta}^{\prime}$, refined parabolically when interior, and $\hat{\tau}(a)$ is $\hat{\tau}\left(\cdot\,;a\right)$ interpolated linearly along the candidate axis at $\hat{\theta}^{\prime}(a)$.
    \item \emph{Closing the state.} Form the residual
    \begin{align}\label{\refeq:esc:aResidual}
        r(a) \equiv a - a_t\left(\hat{\tau}(a),\,h_t\left(\hat{\tau}(a),\hat{\theta}^{\prime}(a)\right);\,\theta_t\right), \qquad a\in\mathcal{G}_{a},
    \end{align}
    with $h_t$ at the off-grid policy interpolated from step 1. Bracket every sign change of $r$ on $\mathcal{G}_{a}$ and close it by a bracketed secant (false position with the Illinois modification, bisection where a step would leave the bracket or the bracket stalls), re-running step 3 at each trial $a$, until the bracket is narrower than $10^{-9}$; it is a root if $\left|r\right|$ there is below $10^{-6}$. A bracket that does not close, where $\hat{\theta}^{\prime}(a)$ jumps between two local maxima of $\mathcal{V}_t$, contains no fixed point. Each closed root is an equilibrium \eqref{\refeq:esc:stateEq}; among several, select the highest $\mathcal{W}_t$ in its own equilibrium, the rule of \cref{\refsec:RobustRoot}, and report the number of brackets and the number closed at every state and period. Should none close, flag the state and value each candidate design at its own consistent distribution there, so that the recursion is not stopped by one state.
    \item \emph{Hand-back.} At the selected root $a^{\ast}$ set $\Theta_t\left(s_{t-1},\theta_t\right) = \hat{\theta}^{\prime}\left(a^{\ast}\right)$ and $\mathcal{T}_t\left(s_{t-1},\theta_t\right) = \hat{\tau}\left(a^{\ast}\right)$; smooth $\mathcal{T}_t$ along $s_{t-1}$ with pinned knots as in \cref{\refalg:CRRA:grid}; re-solve the state at these policies, tabulate $\left(\tau_t,\theta_{t+1},s_t,h_t\right)$ and rebuild the interpolants for period $t-1$. They are piecewise linear along $s_{t-1}$, extrapolating beyond $\mathcal{S}$, and along $\theta_t$ the Catmull-Rom cubic through the four nodes of $\mathcal{G}_{\theta}$ around the query, with the tax clipped to $[l,u]$ and the design to $[0,1]$, since a cubic can overshoot a bound next to a kink or a flat stretch.
\end{enumerate}
\end{alg}

The recursion is one backward pass. Tabulating $r$ on $\mathcal{G}_{a}$, rather than iterating on $a$ from a seed, shows every crossing, and the count says whether the selection among equilibria ever bound; a coarse $\mathcal{G}_{a}$ suffices because $r$ is close to linear, $a_t$ responding to $a$ only through the response of the chosen policy to the distribution it inherits. A multiplicity, where it occurs, is intrinsic (savers who expect a more Bismarckian design save in a way that can make that design attractive), and payoff dominance is the assumption about expectations of \cref{\refsec:RobustRoot}, applied to the design. Steps 2--4 are closed-form passes over the grid of step 1; the state is re-solved once, at the selected policy, which checks the grid reading of $\mathcal{V}_t$. Where a run pins the design as history, $\mathcal{G}_{\theta}^{\prime}$ collapses to the inherited design at those periods; pinning is built into the recursion because $\tau_t$ responds to $\theta_{t+1}$ under CRRA, and pinned everywhere the recursion reduces to the exogenous-design solver of \cref{\refsec:PEE}.

The objective is flat near its maximum, so the reported design is resolved by the candidate grid $\mathcal{G}_{\theta}^{\prime}$ and by the interpolant along $\mathcal{G}_{\theta}$, not by $\mathcal{S}$. The choice reads the continuation between the nodes of $\mathcal{G}_{\theta}$, where the kinks of a piecewise-linear interpolant would set the argmax; hence the Catmull-Rom cubic of step 5, continuously differentiable and exact for quadratics, and not a monotone cubic, whose tangents vanish at every turning point of the nodal data while the anticipated tax turns near the observed design. The results use $41$ candidates, $13$ nodes of $\mathcal{G}_{\theta}$ and $150$ state nodes, and the cost is calibrated by scanning on a $50$-node state grid and refining on the $150$-node one.

\smalltitle{The path iteration.} A cheaper construction approximates \cref{\refalg:esc:crra2D} by solving the equilibrium \emph{path} rather than the policy functions:

\begin{alg}\label{\refalg:esc:crraPath}
\emph{Leaded choice, CRRA preferences, path iteration approximation.} Given a design path $\bm{\theta}$ with $\theta_t$ fixed at the incumbent value for $t\leq t_0$: sweep forward over $t \geq t_0$; at each $t$, evaluate $\mathcal{W}_t$ at every candidate $\theta_{t+1}\in\mathcal{G}_{\theta}^{\prime}$, re-solving the whole politico-economic equilibrium of \cref{\refalg:CRRA:grid} for each candidate while holding $\theta_{t+2},\dots$ at the current iterate; replace $\theta_{t+1}$ by the maximiser. Repeat the sweep until the path stops moving.
\end{alg}

Re-solving at every candidate re-optimises $\tau_t$ at each design, so the tax's own response to $\theta_{t+1}$ contributes nothing to first order. The construction approximates \cref{\refalg:esc:crra2D} on two counts: it holds $\theta_{t+2}$ fixed while $\theta_{t+1}$ varies, which is exact under log preferences by \cref{prop:esc:separability} and an approximation under CRRA; and it values each candidate on its own re-solved path, so that both $a_t$ and $s_{t-1}$ move with the candidate, which \eqref{\refeq:esc:stateEq} rules out and no grid refinement removes. It is held to continuity instead: as $\rho\rightarrow1$ its answer must converge on the log solver's at a first-order rate, the two sharing the weights of \eqref{\refeq:esc:leadedObjective} and nothing else. It enters no result of the paper. The same equilibria can also be located from a first order condition for the design, constructed at a frozen state as the tax condition is; that route needs one further numerical derivative, along the candidate axis through the continuation interpolants, and was not adopted.


\subsubsection{Calibrating the cost}\label{\refsec:ESC:calibration}
Three targets and three parameters. $\left(\beta,\omega\right)$ are calibrated to $\left(R_{t_0},\tau_{t_0}\right)$ exactly as in \cref{\refsec:calibration}, with the cost installed and the design held at the observed $\theta^{\ast}$; and $\lambda$ in \eqref{\refmodeleq:esc:AB} is chosen so that the design \emph{in force} at the baseline year, on a path where the political choice binds from the first period of the horizon, is the observed one,
\begin{align}\label{\refeq:esc:calibration}
    \theta_{t_0} = \Theta_{t_0-1}\left(\theta_{t_0-1}\right) = \theta^{\ast}.
\end{align}
The model's own political history thus delivers the observed system by the baseline year, and everything after $t_0$ is a prediction. Under the variant that keeps $\pi_t = \Pi_t/R_t$ (\cref{\refmodelsec:ESC}), the calibration of $\left(\beta,\omega\right)$ at a fixed $\pi_t$ alternates with $\pi_t$ recomputed on the calibrated path until $\pi_t$ is a fixed point.

\smalltitle{Why the design in force and not the choice made.} The design is a state chosen one period earlier, so $\theta_{t_0}$ and $\Theta_{t_0}(\theta^{\ast})$ differ by one period of $\nu_t$. The counterfactuals are equilibrium paths read at $t_0$ (\cref{\refsec:ESC:counterfactuals}), so the baseline path must sit on the observed design \emph{at} $t_0$. Under log preferences \cref{prop:esc:separability} makes $\Theta_{t_0-1}$ constant in its argument, so \eqref{\refeq:esc:calibration} does not depend on the inherited $\theta_{t_0-1}$; under CRRA the residual is evaluated with the rest of the path held at $\theta^{\ast}$, which is exact at the root.

\smalltitle{The nesting is an approximation.} $\left(\beta,\omega\right)$ are calibrated along the path with the design held fixed, not along the endogenous-design path. At the calibrated cost the two agree at $t_0$ by construction and drift apart only later, so the error is second order, and the solved endogenous path is checked back against the $\left(R,\tau\right)$ targets (\cref{\refsec:ESC:checks}). Under the permanent timing the chosen design equals the incumbent from $t_0$ on at the calibrated cost, so the nesting is exact; its residual is evaluated at the incumbent's ratio in one pass, which shares the root of \eqref{\refeq:esc:permFixedPoint} but not the residual away from it, so a scan must not mix the two.

\smalltitle{Why \eqref{\refeq:esc:calibration} is scanned and not bracketed.} The residual is \emph{flat at a corner}: wherever the choice sits at $\theta=0$ or $\theta=1$ it does not move with $\lambda$, and a root finder handed such a bracket reports spurious convergence at an endpoint. We therefore scan $\lambda$ on a logarithmic grid, set per $\rho$ since the required cost falls steeply in $\rho$, locate a sign change, and only then bracket. A run that finds no interior crossing reports that fact rather than a number, which is the expected outcome for an observed design at the Bismarckian corner: no finite $\lambda$ places the choice at $\theta=1$. Under \eqref{\refmodeleq:esc:AB} the target $\theta^{\ast}$ does not move with $\lambda$ (\cref{\refmodelsec:ESC}), so the residual is a function of the cost alone. The calibrated cost falls with $\rho$ because a higher intertemporal elasticity strengthens the young's forward-looking stake in a Bismarckian design, so less friction is needed to keep the choice off the Beveridgean corner.


\subsubsection{The counterfactuals}\label{\refsec:ESC:counterfactuals}
Every counterfactual, here as in the paper's US tables, is a \emph{separate equilibrium path}, not a shock applied to the calibrated economy at the baseline year: the changed characteristics hold over the whole horizon, the economy begins at its own steady state, the political choice binds from the first period, and the path is read at $t_0$. A row describes a country that has always had this mix of characteristics, mostly American and partly French.

An unanticipated reform at $t_0$, seeded with the savings the baseline generation actually made, would describe an economy surprised in 2020: a coherent object, but not commensurable with France's own calibrated path, which is an equilibrium and not a surprise. A change dated $t_0$ with the design pinned as history through $t_0$ could moreover not move $\theta$ before $t_0+1$, so a table read at $t_0$ would be identical across the exogenous- and endogenous-design readings by construction. Under log preferences the construction matters little: with a Cobb--Douglas technology the tax rate and the savings rate do not depend on the inherited capital stock, so at $\rho=1$ the two constructions differ in the level of hours alone.

Each counterfactual is reported with the design held at the US value and with the design chosen. Alongside them the French tables carry France's own calibrated path, which is not a counterfactual on the American model: France brings its own political weight $\omega$ as well as its own characteristics, so the distance between it and the all-characteristics row measures what the observables leave unexplained. Its workweek is a calibration target, since the French calibration targets French hours relative to American ones (\cref{\refmodelsec:calibration:targets}).


\subsubsection{Checks}\label{\refsec:ESC:checks}
The claims of this subsection and of \cref{\refmodelsec:ESC} are checked by the regression suites of \texttt{python/US} (\texttt{test\_esc.py} unless another is named) and in the result files of \texttt{results/esc/}.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \emph{Nesting.} $\lambda=0$ reproduces the exogenous-design model bitwise, the leaded recursion included.
    \item \emph{The comparison arm.} The comparison wedge's calibrated parameter at $\rho=1$ is pinned, as the gate for every path the cost leaves untouched.
    \item \emph{The costless corner.} The costless leaded and permanent choices are $\theta=0$, and \eqref{\refmodeleq:esc:seqFOC} is negative on $[0,1]$ at every date (at $\rho\neq1$: \texttt{results/numerical/C5\_criticalTurnout.csv}).
    \item \emph{Decoupling and separability.} $z_t$ does not move over the unit square of $\left(\tau_{t+1},\theta_{t+1}\right)$, and the interior leaded choice is invariant to $s_{t-1}$ and to $\theta_t$.
    \item \emph{The retirees' derivative.} \eqref{\refmodeleq:esc:dlnc2i} equals a central difference of $\ln c_{2,t}^i$ at fixed shares, and the factor $1+\tau\,\partial_{\tau}\ln f$ is not negligible.
    \item \emph{$\tilde{V}$ and identification.} \eqref{\refmodeleq:esc:Vtilde} is invariant to common rescalings of $X_i$ and of $\eta_i$ and zero at equal incomes, where $f\equiv1$; $\theta^{\ast}$ does not move with $\lambda$.
    \item \emph{The variant with $\pi_t$.} At $\pi_t=1$ it is \eqref{\refmodeleq:esc:AB}, and every solve returns at its fixed point in $\pi_t$.
    \item \emph{The scan.} One sign change; the free path at the calibrated cost reproduces $\theta^{\ast}$ at $t_0$ and the $\left(R,\tau\right)$ targets, with the drifts in \texttt{escCalibration.csv} and \texttt{escPathCRRA.csv}.
    \item \emph{The log recursion.} Its tax at a fixed design is the exogenous-design solver's, and its design in force at $t_0$ maximises the objective with the anticipated tax solved at every candidate, independently of either design grid (\texttt{test\_gridConvergence.py}).
    \item \emph{The CRRA recursion.} Pinned everywhere, it reproduces the exogenous-design CRRA tax path, and at the suite's calibration its choice agrees with the path iteration, which converges on the log choice at a first-order rate (\texttt{test\_escCRRA.py}); $13$ nodes of $\mathcal{G}_{\theta}$ match $97$ (\texttt{test\_gridConvergence.py}).
    \item \emph{The frozen comparison.} One closed bracket of \eqref{\refeq:esc:aResidual} per state, no fallback, no profitable one-shot deviation at $a^{\ast}$, and the secant closing on the bisection one (\texttt{test\_designChoicePilot.py}); every CRRA row records the design and tax counts (\texttt{test\_counts.py}).
    \item \emph{The permanent timing.} The tax at the choice is $\mathcal{T}_{t_0}$ there, the pinning of the ratio changes the choice, and the fixed point solves its own equation; reference numbers in \texttt{test\_escTiming.py}.
    \item \emph{Placebo.} French leisure, a common rescaling of $X_i$, leaves the chosen design at the baseline's (\texttt{escShocks*.csv}).
    \item \emph{The calibration variant.} The cost and the brackets depend on $y^{\eta}/\Gamma_h$ alone, which both variants match; the endogenous-design results run under common $X$ only.
    \item \emph{Stationary policies.} The misplacement of the design under stationary policy functions: \texttt{results/numerical/US\_ESC\_stationaryApprox.csv}.
\end{enumerate}
