\section{Endogenous system characteristics}\label{app:ESC}
The following explores different ways to endogenize the choice of $\theta$; all instances rely on the simplest log-model without informal households. 

\textit{New high-level results:} Sequential, leaded, and permanent choices of $\theta$ all end up with the corner solution $\theta = 0$. Only the specification with of raising public funds yield an interior solution. 


\subsection{Sequential choice of $\theta$}
In the first version, we assume that $\theta_t$ is determined the same way as taxes as sequential outcomes of an agent maximizing the political objective function. In this case, marginal effect of $\theta_t$ on the political objective is simply given by 
\begin{align*}
   \dfrac{\partial \mathcal{W}_t}{\partial \theta_t} &= \omega\sum_i\gamma_i \overbrace{\dfrac{\partial \ln\left(\Theta_{c_2,t}^i\right)}{\partial \theta_t}}^{\equiv \mathcal{E}_{2,t}^{i, \theta}} \\
   &=\omega \sum_i\gamma_i\frac{\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\frac{\tau_t}{1+\gamma_0\epsilon}\left(\frac{\eta_{i}^{1+\xi}}{X_{i}^{\xi}}\frac{1}{\Gamma_{h}}-1\right)}{\frac{s_{t-1}^i}{s_{t-1}}+\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\frac{\tau_t}{1+\gamma_0\epsilon}\left(1+\theta_t\left[\frac{\eta_{i}^{1+\xi}}{X_{i}^{\xi}}\frac{1}{\Gamma_{h}}-1\right]\right)}
\end{align*}
It is straightforward to verify that $\partial \mathcal{W}_t/\partial \theta_t\leq0$ for $\theta_t\in[0,1]$; thus the choice is always in a corner with $\theta_t=0$.\footnote{The numerator averages to zero, while the denominator is larger (more positive) for high income groups.} For the current retirees, lowering $\theta_t$ constitutes a redistribution from consumers with low marginal utility to consumers with high marginal utility. Because there are no strategic effects through current labor supply (the choice of $h_t$ depends on $\theta_{t+1}$), this always implies a corner solution. 


\subsection{Permanent choice of $\theta$}
Assume that deviations in $\theta$ are costly to implement and the expectation there is, that a current implementation of a new $\theta$ value is permanent. In this case, the new $\theta$ value becomes a relevant state in this specific year where the change happens, because it influences the relative savings rates in $t+1$. However, interestingly, the value of $\tau_t$ still does not have a direct impact on any endogenous states in $t+1$. 


In this case, the marginal effect of $\theta$ on the indirect utility of current retirees is given by:
\begin{align*}
    \dfrac{d v_{2,t}^i}{d \theta} = (1-\alpha)\frac{d\ln\left(\Theta_{h,t}\right)}{d\theta} + \mathcal{E}_{2,t}^{i,\theta},
\end{align*}
where $\mathcal{E}_{2,t}^{i,\theta}$ is the effect from the sequential choice of $\theta$. 


For the current young generation with indirect utility $v_{1,t}^i$, the effect of $\theta$ is given by (see section \ref{app:PEE:CRRA}):
\begin{align*}
    \dfrac{d v_{1,t}^i}{d\theta} = (1+\beta) \frac{d\ln\left(\tilde{c}_{1,t}^i\right)}{d\theta} + \beta \ln\left(\frac{R_{t+1}}{d\theta}\right). 
\end{align*}
The derivatives includes the direct effect of changing $\theta$ through economic equilibrium functions and the indirect effects working through different policy choices in all $t>t_0$. 


We identify the politico-economic equilibrium by first identifying $N$ different paths for different permanent levels of $\theta$. Then, for the specific date $t_0$ for the permanent choice of $\theta$, we create a two-dimensional grid of solution candidates for choices of both $\tau_{t_0}, \theta$. For each candidate $\theta$ on the grid, we incorporate that future solution paths then follow this trajectory permanently. 

On this grid of solution candidates, we can approximate relevant derivatives directly using the gridded solutions. We can exploit that policy choices are independent of the level of $s_{t-1}$ and simply assume that $s_{t-1} = 1$, then solve for relevant terms like $\tilde{c}_{1,t}^i, R_{t+1}$, and $h_t$, and then approximate gradients directly.\footnote{We can use this to verify our conjecture that the arbitrary level of $s_{t-1}$ indeed does not have any effect on the results by changing this and testing the outcomes.}


\bigskip
With the current level of inequality we observe in the US data, however, the endogenous choice of $\theta$ predicts the corner solution of $\theta = 0$, even with high levels of labor supply elasticities. However, with modest reductions in income inequality, we can calibrate the permanent choice model to the observed choice of $\theta$ in 2020. Specifically, if we add 25\% to the low income group and reduce high income group by 25\%, we can calibrate the model with a $\xi \approx 0.41$.

\subsection{Leaded choices of $\theta_t$}
Assume now instead that $\theta_t$ is chosen in period $t-1$. In this case, $\theta_t$ will be a relevant state variable in $t$ as it directly affects the marginal effect of choosing $\tau_t$ for retirees. 

For the current retirees, the effect of $\theta_{h+1}$ only affects the indirect utility of current retirees. The marginal effect is given by
\begin{align*}
    \frac{dv_{2,t}^i}{d\theta_{t+1}} &= (1-\alpha)\frac{d\ln\left(\Theta_{h,t}\right)}{d\theta_{t+1}},
\end{align*}
where the impact on $h_t$ is both directly through changes in $\theta_{t+1}$, but also indirectly through anticipated changes in $\tau_{t+1}$.

For the current young generation, recall that we can write the indirect utility as $v_{1,t}^i = (1+\beta)\ln(\tilde{c}_{1,t}^i)+\beta \ln(\beta R_{t+1})$. In this case, the marginal effect of $\theta_{t+1}$ on the indirect utility is then given by
\begin{align*}
    \dfrac{dv_{1,t}^i}{d\theta_{t+1}} = (1+\beta) \frac{d \ln\left(\Theta_{\tilde{c}_1,t}^i\right)}{d\theta_{t+1}} + \beta \frac{d \ln(R_{t+1})}{d\theta_{t+1}}
\end{align*}

For labor supply:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $\theta_{t+1}$ increases --> $h_t$ increases (EE effect).
    \item $\theta_{t+1}$ increases --> $\tau_{t+1}$ decreases --> $h_t$ decreases (PEE effect).
    \item Net effect (for US calibration) --> $h_t$ increases. 
\end{itemize}

For savings:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $\theta_{t+1}$ increases --> $s_t$ decreases (EE effect). 
    \item $\theta_{t+1}$ increases --> $\tau_{t+1}$ decreases --> $s_t$ increases (PEE effect). 
    \item Net effect (for US calibration) --> $s_t$ increases.
\end{itemize}

In this case, the political choice $\theta_{t+1}$ in period $t$ becomes a relevant state for the subsequent choice of policies in $t+1$. Thus, we identify this with similar approaches as outlined in appendix \ref{app:numeric}. 

With this implementation, we similarly end up in a corner solution $\theta = 0$ with the current level of US inequality for reasonable ranges of $\xi$. 




\subsection{Marginal costs of raising redistributive, public funds}
Assume that the marginal costs of raising public funds for redistributive purposes is higher. Formally, we reformulate the balanced budget of the government to read
\begin{align*}
    \nu_t w_t\tau_th_t f(\theta_t) = \sum_i \gamma_i b_t^i,
\end{align*}
where $f(\theta_t) \in[0,1]$ and $f'(\theta)>0$ implying that there may be an effective wedge between government revenue and received benefits and, importantly, that this wedge is larger for redistributive/Beveridgean benefits. If this is the case, we have
\begin{align*}
    \overline{b}_t = f(\theta_t) \frac{\nu_t w_t h_t \tau_t}{h_{t-1}}.
\end{align*}
Furthermore, discounted benefits in equilibrium (using factor price definitions) gives:\footnote{Essentially, we can repeat all functions only with an added $f(\theta)$ in front of the $(1-\alpha)/\alpha$ part.}
\begin{align*}
    \frac{\overline{b}_{t+1}}{R_{t+1}} = f(\theta_{t+1})\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\frac{s_t}{h_t} \tau_{t+1}
\end{align*}

It is straightforward to verify, going through the same steps as in appendix \ref{app:EE}, that the economic equilibrium can be represented in the same way only with slightly adjusted parameter functions:
\begin{align*}
    \Gamma_{s,t} &= \frac{1}{1+\xi}\frac{\Gamma_h \beta }{1+\beta+\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\tau_{t+1}f(\theta_{t+1})\left(1+\theta_{t+1} \beta\right)} \\
    \Theta_{h,t} &= \left(\Gamma_{h}\right)^{\frac{1+\xi}{1+\alpha\xi}}\left(\frac{(1-\alpha)(1-\tau_t)}{\Gamma_{h}-\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}f(\theta_{t+1})\tau_{t+1}\theta_{t+1}\Gamma_{s,t}}\right),
\end{align*}
and with relative savings defined as
\begin{align*}
    \frac{s_{t,i}}{s_t} = \frac{\eta_i^{1+\xi}}{X_i^{\xi}} \frac{1}{\Gamma_h} + \frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}f(\theta_{t+1})\tau_{t+1}\frac{1-\theta_{t+1}}{1+\beta}\left(\frac{\eta_i^{1+\xi}}{X_i^{\xi}\Gamma_h}-1\right).
\end{align*}
Similar extensions are straightforward to show for consumption functions. 

In this case, the marginal effect of $\theta_t$ still only works through the current retirees; the first order condition then yields
\begin{align*}
    \frac{\partial \mathcal{W}_t}{\partial \theta_t} &= \omega \sum_i\gamma_i\frac{\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\frac{\tau_t}{1+\gamma_0\epsilon}\left[f'(\theta_t)+\left(\theta_t f'(\theta_t)+f(\theta_t)\right)\left(\frac{\eta_{i}^{1+\xi}}{X_{i}^{\xi}}\frac{1}{\Gamma_{h}}-1\right)\right]}{\frac{s_{t-1}^i}{s_{t-1}}+f(\theta_t)\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\frac{\tau_t}{1+\gamma_0\epsilon}\left(1+\theta_t\left[\frac{\eta_{i}^{1+\xi}}{X_{i}^{\xi}}\frac{1}{\Gamma_{h}}-1\right]\right)}
\end{align*}

With $f(\theta_t) = \phi + (1-\phi) \theta_t^p$, the terms become
\begin{align*}
    f'(\theta) = (1-\phi) p \theta_t^{p-1}, \qquad \theta f'(\theta)+f(\theta) = \phi + (1-\phi)\theta_t^p\left(1+p\right)
\end{align*}


We note that the introduction of deadweight losses from raising public funds changes the model in a couple of ways. Importantly, some of the characteristics from proposition \ref{prop:LOG:PEE}, e.g. that equilibrium taxes are decreasing in $\theta_t$ is no longer valid. Instead, it depends on parameters.

In the following, we compare our results from the main section ("Main spec.) with two alternatives: First, the effect of the shock with the new formulation including deadweight losses, but exogenous $\theta_t$ ("Exogenous $\theta$"), and the full effect with endogenous $\theta$ ("Endogenous $\theta_t$). We present results for a model with $\phi = 0.5$ and where $p = 0.41$ is calibrated to target $\theta_t$ in 2020.\footnote{We calibrate both the US and French model separately, giving slightly different calibrated values of $p$. This does not seem to drive results, though.} The choice of $\phi$ is not negligible; Lower values of $\phi$ generally amplifies the effect of shocks to the choice of $\tau_t$, in particular for choices that lower $\theta_t$ significantly. For $\phi$ in the neighborhood of $0.5-0.75$, the results are similar, with slightly more muted results in the upper region of this interval.

Table \ref{table:US_ESC:ageing} compares the effect of "acute ageing" in the US with/without endogenous $\theta_t$.
