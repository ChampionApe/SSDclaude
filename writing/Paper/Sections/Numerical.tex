\section{Numerical Methods}\label{sec:numerical}

This section describes how the politico-economic equilibria of sections \ref{sec:argentina} to \ref{sec:esc} are computed, with the algorithms and the code in the technical documentation \parencite{BergGE26doc}. The quantitative politico-economic literature computes a time-invariant policy function \parencite{KrusellRR99a1,Forni05a1,Song12a1} or compares steady states across demographic scenarios \parencite{Galasso99a1}. Along a demographic transition the Markov policy function is date-specific, since the policymaker at $t$ faces its own mix of cohorts, $\nu_t$, and a continuation policy set further along the transition. The distinction is immaterial in the equilibrium of proposition \ref{prop:LOG:PEE}, which carries no state, but not under CRRA preferences or with informal savers, where the continuation reaches the tax through the state, or in the design choice, where a design chosen at $t$ is weighed against the electorate of $t+1$.\footnote{Solved at each date's own $\nu_t$ and walked along the projected path, a stationary policy function misses the exact tax by at most 0.2 p.p.\ in the U.S.\ economy and 0.3 p.p.\ in Argentina. It misplaces the chosen design of section \ref{sec:esc} by up to $0.053$ under log and $0.059$ under CRRA preferences, against counterfactual responses of $0.030$ to $0.477$.} We therefore compute date-specific policy functions by backward induction from a date $T$ (appendix \ref{app:T}), the end of a finite horizon or the start of a steady state in an infinite one, where conditions i and ii of definition \ref{def:PEE} combine into one fixed point in a time-autonomous policy function. For a large $T$ the two agree away from $T$. We report the finite-horizon solution, which approximates the Markov equilibrium in the limit of finite-horizon economies, the class in which \textcite{mkvss08} establish uniqueness.\footnote{Periods are thirty years; the horizon is 1960--2200 for the rich economies and 1920--2190 for Argentina, with population growth held at its last projected rate after 2080 (2070 for Argentina).}

The state of the political problem is at most two scalars. Parts ii and iii of proposition \ref{prop:LOG:EE} keep the distributions of hours and formal savings out of it, and under log preferences the political objective is log-separable in aggregate savings and hours, so, with the design given, the model without informal households has none. Informal savers add the ratio $s_{t-1,0}/s_{t-1}$ of informal to formal savings, which depends on past policy through $\tau_{t-1}$ and on the current tax, which savers anticipated (appendix \ref{app:EE:CRRA}); appendix \ref{app:PEE:LOG_s0} derives the resulting first order condition. CRRA preferences add aggregate savings $s_{t-1}$ (appendix \ref{app:PEE:CRRA}). At each state the first order condition in $\tau_t$, which holds the distribution of past savings fixed, is evaluated on a grid of taxes, and the corners and interior roots are ranked by the political objective reconstructed by integrating it along the grid; terms involving the continuation policy are differentiated numerically.

Each reported path starts from the steady state under the first period's own policy, a scalar fixed point, with the economic equilibrium re-solved exactly at the taxes the policy functions deliver. Calibration nests the path in a search over the parameters that only the equilibrium pins down, until the targets of sections \ref{sec:argentina} and \ref{sec:oecd} hold in the calibration year.

In the design choice of section \ref{sec:esc}, log preferences make each date's tax a scalar problem given the designs in force and leave the design chosen at $t$ independent of the savings state and of the design inherited, so each date's choice is a one-dimensional search over a grid of designs. Under CRRA preferences neither property survives: the equilibrium is a backward recursion over policy functions of the state $(s_{t-1},\theta_t)$, in which the objective is flat near its maximum, so the chosen design is reported to the resolution of the grid. Section \ref{sec:argentina} first compares the model with an observed reform in Argentina.
