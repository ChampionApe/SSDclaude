\section{Endogenous pension design under ageing}\label{app:US:escTables}\setcounter{equation}{0}
This appendix reports the one counterfactual of section \ref{sec:esc} whose table the paper keeps, the endogenous design under ageing. The counterfactuals of section \ref{sec:oecd} under general CRRA preferences and under the alternative calibration of the taste for leisure, the same exercise with the UK as the host economy, and the counterfactuals of section \ref{sec:esc} that impose France's characteristics are in \oa{oecd-us}.

Table \ref{table:US_ESC:ageing} reports the acute-ageing counterfactual of section \ref{sec:esc} with the design pinned at the U.S.\ value and with the design chosen, at $\rho \in \{0.5, 1, 2\}$; figure \ref{fig:US_ESC:overview} collects it with the counterfactuals that impose France's characteristics. The chosen design rises from $0.738$ to $0.816$ at $\rho = 0.5$ and to $0.825$ and $0.826$ at $\rho = 1$ and $2$, so the response to ageing hardly depends on the elasticity, and the tax rate ends between $0.1$ and $0.3$ p.p.\ above the pinned reading, since a more Bismarckian system dissipates less of its revenue and is worth a larger tax to the electorate. The additional fall in the savings rate is at most $0.4$ p.p.\ of GDP, and the workweek is unchanged to a tenth of an hour.
