# data

Inputs to the models and to figure 1; nothing here is a result. The workbooks are edited in Excel, not
through openpyxl (the root `README.md`, traps). A source marked *to add* is recorded nowhere in the repository
yet; the paper's calibration appendices (E for Argentina, F for the U.S., France and the UK) name the
surveys and the targets in prose.

| File | Content | Source and vintage | Read by | In the paper |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `ArgentinaTest.xlsx` | `population`: workers per retiree by model period from 1920. `heterogeneity`: the informal type ($\gamma_0 = 0.4706$, informal households per formal household, the model's convention, so that they are 32% of all households: the share of the elderly without a pension in 2004, Rofman and Oliveri 2012; 0.32 until 2026-10-06, when it counted 24%) and four formal income quartiles, with relative income, hours, voting shares (equal) and survival. `calibration`: the 2010 targets (capital share 0.35, Frisch elasticity 0.30, pension spending 7.1% of GDP, savings rate 16.5%, replacement-rate ratio 0.844 between groups 1 and 3, workweek 42.5 h). | Encuesta Permanente de Hogares 2010 (INDEC) for income, hours and shares; OECD replacement rates; ANSES and Cetrángolo and Grushka for spending; census growth rates and projections for $\nu_t$ (appendix E).
