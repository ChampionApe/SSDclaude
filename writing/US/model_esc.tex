\subsection{Endogenous system characteristics}\label{\refsec:ESC}
Everything up to this point treats the pension design $\theta_t$ as a parameter, calibrated from the observed dispersion of replacement rates. This section makes it a political outcome. Two ingredients are needed, and they are logically independent: a \emph{cost} that keeps the choice off the boundary, and a \emph{timing} convention that says when the design is chosen and what it is expected to bind for.

The cost matters because without one the choice is degenerate. Under any of the timings below, $\theta_t$ redistributes a fixed pot among retirees whose marginal utilities differ, and a utilitarian aggregator with equal weights always prefers the flat system: the political objective is monotone and the equilibrium is the corner $\theta_t = 0$. This is not a numerical accident but a property of the objective, and it survives every timing convention we have examined.


\smalltitle{The deadweight cost of redistributive benefits.}
The flat component of a pay-as-you-go benefit taxes some contributors and subsidises others, and we make it costly for exactly that reason, in a reduced form that leaves the structure of the model intact. Write the benefit formula as
\begin{align}\label{\refeq:esc:benefits}
    b_t^i = \left[A(\theta_t,\tau_t)h_{t-1,i}\eta_{t-1,i} + B(\theta_t,\tau_t)h_{t-1}\right]\overline{b}_t,
\end{align}
with $\overline{b}_t$ still given by \eqref{\refeq:governmentBudget:bbar}. The baseline model is $\left(A,B\right) = \left(\theta,1-\theta\right)$, where the two coefficients sum to one and \eqref{\refeq:esc:benefits} reduces to the formula in \eqref{\refeq:governmentBudget}. With a cost they sum to less than one, and the form of the shortfall is derived rather than assumed.

\emph{The implicit tax.} Let $y_{t,i}\equiv y_{t,i}^{\eta}/\Gamma_{h,t}$ denote the labour income of type $i$ relative to the mean --- the $z_i^{\eta}$ of \eqref{\refeq:calibration:zeta} at vintage $t$ --- so that $\eta_{t,i}h_{t,i}/h_t = y_{t,i}$ by \eqref{\refeq:EE:hi} and $\sum_i\gamma_{t,i}y_{t,i}=1$. A retiree of type $i$ at $t$ draws $\left[\theta_t y_{t-1,i}+1-\theta_t\right]h_{t-1}\overline{b}_t$ on past earnings $w_{t-1}y_{t-1,i}h_{t-1}$; in expectation at the time of contributing, this is $\Pi_t\tau_t\left[\theta_t + (1-\theta_t)/y_{t-1,i}\right]$ per unit of those earnings, where $\Pi_t\equiv p_{t-1}\overline{b}_t/(\tau_tw_{t-1}) = \nu_tw_th_t/(w_{t-1}h_{t-1})$ by \eqref{\refeq:governmentBudget:bbar} is the pay-as-you-go return factor: the benefit per unit of earnings that a purely earnings-related system pays out of the same revenue. The flat component moves type $i$ away from that benchmark by
\begin{align}\label{\refeq:esc:implicitTax}
    t_{t,i} \equiv \Pi_t\,\tau_t\,(1-\theta_t)\left(1-\frac{1}{y_{t-1,i}}\right)
\end{align}
per unit of earnings: a tax on types above mean income, a subsidy to types below it, and a pure transfer in the aggregate, since $\sum_i\gamma_{t-1,i}y_{t-1,i}t_{t,i}=0$. This is the \emph{tax component} of the contribution in the sense of Summers (1989) and Disney (2004) --- the part of the payroll charge that buys the contributor no benefit, and the part whose employment effects Disney documents across the OECD.

\emph{The Harberger loss.} A set of proportional wedges $t_i$ on bases $e_i$ with a common compensated elasticity $\varepsilon$ costs $\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon\sum_i\gamma_it_i^2e_i$ to second order in the wedges. Per unit of mean past earnings the bases are $e_i = y_{t-1,i}$, so the loss is $\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon\,\Pi_t^2\tau_t^2(1-\theta_t)^2\tilde{V}_{t-1}$ with
\begin{align}\label{\refeq:esc:Vtilde}
    \tilde{V}_{t} \equiv \sum_i\gamma_{t,i}\frac{\left(y_{t,i}-1\right)^2}{y_{t,i}},
\end{align}
while revenue per unit of mean past earnings is $\Pi_t\tau_t$. As a share of revenue the loss is therefore $\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon\,\Pi_t\,\tau_t\,(1-\theta_t)^2\,\tilde{V}_{t-1}$: quadratic in the share of the system that is flat, linear in its size, and proportional to a Harberger-weighted dispersion of relative income that is zero when all incomes are equal and, being a function of $y^{\eta}/\Gamma_h$ alone, invariant to the normalisations of \eqref{\refeq:scaleInvariance} and \eqref{\refeq:hoursUnit}. The same tax component is the mechanism by which Koethenbuerger, Poutvaara and Profeta (2008) account for the stylised fact of Conde-Ruiz and Profeta (2007) that more redistributive systems are smaller; here it is read from the other side, as what makes a larger system costlier to keep redistributive.

\emph{Specification.} We adopt the exponential form
\begin{align}\label{\refeq:esc:AB}
    f(\theta_t,\tau_t) &\equiv \exp\left(-\tfrac{1}{2}\,\lambda\,\tau_t\,\tilde{V}_{t-1}\,(1-\theta_t)^2\right), &
    \left(A,B\right) &= \left(f(\theta_t,\tau_t)\,\theta_t,\; f(\theta_t,\tau_t)\,(1-\theta_t)\right),
\end{align}
so that the whole system carries the cost, the surviving share is $A+B=f\in(0,1]$, and $1-f$ is the share of revenue that does not arrive; to first order it is the Harberger share, with $\lambda$ in the place of $\varepsilon\Pi_t$.\footnote{The return factor multiplies every implicit tax in \eqref{\refeq:esc:implicitTax} and so scales the loss share once. It carries $\nu_t$ and would make a structural cost parameter drift with demographics; we absorb it into $\lambda$, which is calibrated at the baseline date rather than derived. The $\lambda$ of this section is the cost parameter of the paper and is unrelated to the rescaling factor that \eqref{\refeq:scaleInvariance} happens to call by the same letter.} The exponential keeps $f$ positive for every $\lambda$ and every size of system, nests the baseline at $\lambda=0$, and is \emph{proportional}: the cost scales both components alike and cancels from every ratio of benefits. Three properties come with the derivation. Nothing is lost when nothing is redistributed: $f=1$ at $\theta_t=1$, and $f=1$ for every design when $\tilde{V}_{t-1}=0$, where the two designs pay identical benefits. The loss per unit of redistribution rises with the size of the system, through $\tau_t$. And the marginal cost of the first unit of redistribution is zero: $\partial_{\theta}\ln f = \lambda\tau_t\tilde{V}_{t-1}(1-\theta_t)$ vanishes at $\theta_t=1$, so under a purely quadratic loss a corner at $\theta_t=1$ is never chosen; a linear term in $1-\theta_t$ would restore that possibility at the price of a second parameter, and we do not introduce one. The government's budget is unchanged in form: it collects $\nu_t w_t \tau_t h_t$ and delivers $\left(A+B\right)h_{t-1}\overline{b}_tp_{t-1}$ per contributor, so $\overline{b}_t$ should be read as \emph{gross} revenue per unit of past labour and the fraction $1-f$ as the resources that do not arrive.

\emph{Dates.} $f$ takes the design and the tax of the date whose benefits it scales: $(\theta_t,\tau_t)$ in \eqref{\refeq:esc:benefits} and in the consumption of current retirees; $(\theta_{t+1},\tau_{t+1})$ wherever the young anticipate their own benefits --- in $\Gamma_{s,t}$, $\Theta_{h,t}$, $s_{t,i}/s_t$ and the bracket of $\tilde{c}_{1,t}^i$ --- with $\tau_{t+1} = \mathcal{T}_{t+1}(\theta_{t+1})$ the continuation tax, so that the young at $t$ anticipate both the design and the size of the system that will pay them, which is the Harberger reading. $\tilde{V}$ carries the vintage of the cohort whose benefits the bracket prices, $\tilde{V}_{t-1}$ in the benefit formula and $\tilde{V}_t$ in the terms of the young; with a time-invariant productivity distribution the two coincide, and the vintages are kept apart so that a time-varying one would not silently mix them.

Two earlier reduced forms, $f(\theta) = \phi + (1-\phi)\theta^{p}$ applied to both components or to the flat one alone, attached the cost to the design rather than to the transfer the design performs, and so burned a share $1-\phi$ of revenue at $\theta=0$ even when all incomes were equal; the proportional one is kept as a comparison arm in \cref{\refnumsec:ESC}. What the present form keeps from them is the position on magnitude. The shape of \eqref{\refeq:esc:AB} follows the deadweight-loss logic of the tax component; its size does not. The calibrated $\lambda$ (\cref{\refnumsec:ESC:calibration}) is an order of magnitude above what compensated labour-supply elasticities of $0.1$ to $0.6$ would give, and stands in for administrative, evasion and political costs of redistribution that the model does not contain. We do not take a stand on their microfoundation, and the reduced form is compatible with several: administrative cost that scales with the redistributive component, an avoidance or informality margin that responds to the part of the contribution that is a pure tax rather than deferred pay, or imperfect targeting of a means-tested benefit. The paper's own Argentine model contains a formality margin of exactly this kind, which is the closest structural analogue available to us.


\smalltitle{What the cost changes, and what it does not.}
Very little of the model needs rewriting, for a reason worth stating explicitly.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:esc:substitution}
In every equation of the economic equilibrium, the design enters only through the two groups $\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\tau_{t+1}\theta_{t+1}$ and $\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\tau_{t+1}(1-\theta_{t+1})$ (and their date-$t$ counterparts in the benefit formula). The cost is therefore \emph{exactly} the substitution $\theta_{t+1} \mapsto A(\theta_{t+1},\tau_{t+1})$ and $(1-\theta_{t+1})\mapsto B(\theta_{t+1},\tau_{t+1})$ in those groups, and $\theta_t \mapsto A(\theta_t,\tau_t)$, $(1-\theta_t)\mapsto B(\theta_t,\tau_t)$ in the benefit formula, and changes nothing else in the economic equilibrium. In the political equilibrium it adds one factor: because $A$ and $B$ depend on the tax that funds them, the term of the retired in the tax first order condition picks up $\partial_{\tau}\ln f$.
\end{proposition}

The claim can be read off \eqref{\refnumeq:auxiliary}: $\theta_{t+1}$ appears in $\Gamma_{s,t}$, $\Theta_{h,t}$ and $s_{t,i}/s_t$ and nowhere else, and always in one of the two groups. Applying the substitution,
\begin{subequations}\label{\refeq:esc:auxiliary}
\begin{align}
    \Gamma_{s,t} &= \frac{1}{1+\xi}\frac{\sum_i \gamma_{t,i}y_{t,i}^{\eta}\frac{B_{t+1}^i}{1+B_{t+1}^i}}{1+\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\tau_{t+1}\left(A(\theta_{t+1},\tau_{t+1})+B(\theta_{t+1},\tau_{t+1})\sum_i\frac{\gamma_{t,i}}{1+B_{t+1}^i}\right)},
    \label{\refeq:esc:auxiliary:Gammas} \\
    \Theta_{h,t} &= \left(\Gamma_{h,t}\right)^{\frac{1+\xi}{1+\alpha\xi}}\left(\frac{(1-\alpha)(1-\tau_t)}{\Gamma_{h,t}-\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}A(\theta_{t+1},\tau_{t+1})\tau_{t+1}\Gamma_{s,t}}\right)^{\frac{\xi}{1+\alpha\xi}},
    \label{\refeq:esc:auxiliary:Thetah}
\end{align}
and, in the log case where $B_{t+1}^i = \beta$ is common,
\begin{align}
    \frac{s_{t,i}}{s_t} &= \frac{y_{t,i}^{\eta}}{\Gamma_{h,t}} + \frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\frac{B(\theta_{t+1},\tau_{t+1})\tau_{t+1}}{1+\beta}\left(\frac{y_{t,i}^{\eta}}{\Gamma_{h,t}}-1\right).
    \label{\refeq:esc:auxiliary:si}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
Second-period consumption picks up the substitution at date $t$ rather than $t+1$: the bracket $1+\theta_t\left(y_{t-1,i}-1\right)$ appearing in \eqref{\refeq:EE:ci} becomes $A(\theta_t,\tau_t)\,y_{t-1,i} + B(\theta_t,\tau_t)$. The two coincide when $A+B=1$, so the baseline is nested exactly and not merely as a limit. The one place where the cost does more than substitute is the term of the retired in the tax first order condition, \eqref{\refeq:PEELOG:v2i} and its CRRA counterpart in \eqref{\refeq:PEE}: the bracket there is multiplied by $\tau_t$, and $f$ now moves with $\tau_t$, so
\begin{align}\label{\refeq:esc:dlnc2i}
    \frac{d\ln\left(c_{2,t}^i\right)}{d\tau_t} = (1-\alpha)\frac{d\ln\left(h_t\right)}{d\tau_t} + \frac{\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\left[A(\theta_t,\tau_t)\,y_{t-1,i}+B(\theta_t,\tau_t)\right]\left(1+\tau_t\,\partial_{\tau}\ln f(\theta_t,\tau_t)\right)}{\frac{s_{t-1,i}}{s_{t-1}}+\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\tau_t\left[A(\theta_t,\tau_t)\,y_{t-1,i}+B(\theta_t,\tau_t)\right]},
\end{align}
where $\partial_{\tau}\ln f(\theta_t,\tau_t) = -\tfrac{1}{2}\lambda\tilde{V}_{t-1}(1-\theta_t)^2$, so that $1+\tau_t\partial_{\tau}\ln f = 1+\ln f(\theta_t,\tau_t)$ because $\ln f$ is linear in $\tau_t$: a larger system is worth less to a retiree at the margin than the pot it collects, by the share of the marginal contribution that the flat component dissipates. Nothing else in the tax first order condition sees $f$ under log preferences --- the term of the young, \eqref{\refeq:PEELOG:v1i}, contains no policy but $\tau_t$, and the labour-supply derivative \eqref{\refeq:PEELOG:dThetah} is free of the groups --- and under CRRA the numerical derivatives of the utilities of the young see $f(\theta_{t+1},\tau_{t+1})$ only through the continuation, as they already see $\theta_{t+1}$ and $\tau_{t+1}$.

A consequence of \cref{prop:esc:substitution} that matters for the propositions below: the cost does \emph{not} disturb the decoupling result of \eqref{\refeq:PEELOG:decoupling}. The first order condition for $\tau_t$ still involves $\theta_{t+1}$ only through the level of $\Theta_{h,t}$, which reaches it only through the old informal household --- absent here --- and the new factor in \eqref{\refeq:esc:dlnc2i} is dated $t$, so $z_t$ remains a function of $\left(\tau_t,\theta_t\right)$ alone.


\smalltitle{Identification of $\theta$ is not innocent.}
The replacement-rate ratio that identifies $\theta$ in \eqref{\refeq:calibration:theta} pins the \emph{ratio} of the two coefficients in \eqref{\refeq:esc:benefits} and nothing else:
\begin{align}\label{\refeq:esc:RR}
    \frac{B(\theta,\tau)}{A(\theta,\tau)} = \frac{1-RR_0}{RR_0\,h_1 - h_2}, \qquad h_j \equiv \frac{\Gamma_{h,t_0}X_{t_0,j}^{\xi}}{\eta_{t_0,j}^{1+\xi}},
\end{align}
where $j=1,2$ index the half-mean and mean-income groups. Under the proportional specification \eqref{\refeq:esc:AB} the common factor $f(\theta,\tau)$ cancels from \eqref{\refeq:esc:RR}, so $\theta$ is the same number as in the exogenous-design model whatever the cost parameter --- $0.738$ for the United States --- and the design and the cost are identified separately. This is a property of the specification and not of the exercise: a cost attached to the flat component alone, $(A,B) = (\theta,\, f\,(1-\theta))$, leaves $B/A = (1-\theta)f/\theta$, so that the same datum implies a different $\theta$ for every value of the cost parameter and the two are jointly identified. The object being matched is $RR_0$, not any particular value of $\theta$.


\subsubsection{Three timings}\label{\refsec:ESC:timing}
Let $\mathcal{W}_t$ denote the political objective of \cref{\refsec:PEELOG}, evaluated with the same weights that determine $\tau_t$. We consider three conventions for when the design is chosen. Throughout, $s_{t-1,i}/s_{t-1}$ is \emph{predetermined} at $t$: the electorate takes the inherited distribution of savings as given, exactly as it does when choosing $\tau_t$.

\smalltitle{Sequential.} $\theta_t$ is chosen at $t$ alongside $\tau_t$, and binds for that period only. Because benefits at $t$ are paid on labour supplied at $t-1$, and because current labour supply responds to $\theta_{t+1}$ rather than $\theta_t$, the design has no effect on any date-$t$ quantity other than the split of a fixed pot among retirees. Writing $A'$ and $B'$ for the partial derivatives of $A(\theta_t,\tau_t)$ and $B(\theta_t,\tau_t)$ with respect to $\theta_t$ at the given $\tau_t$, the marginal effect is therefore
\begin{align}\label{\refeq:esc:seqFOC}
    \dfrac{\partial \mathcal{W}_t}{\partial \theta_t} = \omega\sum_i\gamma_{t-1,i}p_{t-1}\mu_{t-1,i}\,
    \frac{\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\tau_t\left[A'(\theta_t,\tau_t)\frac{y_{t-1,i}^{\eta}}{\Gamma_{h,t-1}} + B'(\theta_t,\tau_t)\right]}
         {\frac{s_{t-1,i}}{s_{t-1}}+\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\tau_t\left[A(\theta_t,\tau_t)\frac{y_{t-1,i}^{\eta}}{\Gamma_{h,t-1}}+B(\theta_t,\tau_t)\right]},
\end{align}
with, from \eqref{\refeq:esc:AB}, $A' = f + \theta_t f_{\theta}$ and $B' = (1-\theta_t)f_{\theta} - f$, where $f_{\theta} = f\,\lambda\tau_t\tilde{V}_{t-1}(1-\theta_t)$. Without a cost, $A' = 1$ and $B' = -1$, the numerator is proportional to $y_{t-1,i}^{\eta}/\Gamma_{h,t-1}-1$, which averages to zero across types under the population weights alone, while the denominator is increasing in the same object. With the voting weights $\mu_{t-1,i}$ in the sum the average is not zero (they rise with income in the US calibration), so the sign is established numerically rather than by the argument above: evaluated on the solved baseline path, the sum is negative for every $\theta_t\in[0,1]$ at every dated period, under log preferences and at $\rho = 0.5$ and $2$ (\cref{\refnumsec:ESC}), and the choice is the corner $\theta_t=0$: lowering $\theta$ moves resources from retirees with low marginal utility to retirees with high marginal utility, and nothing offsets it. With a cost, $A'+B' = f_{\theta} = f\,\lambda\tau_t\tilde{V}_{t-1}(1-\theta_t)\geq 0$, positive away from $\theta_t=1$ and zero there, and the sign is no longer determined by the distribution alone.

\smalltitle{Leaded.} $\theta_{t+1}$ is chosen at $t$ and becomes a state at $t+1$. The design now reaches current labour supply, through $A(\theta_{t+1},\tau_{t+1})$ and $B(\theta_{t+1},\tau_{t+1})$ in \eqref{\refeq:esc:auxiliary}, and through it every date-$t$ quantity, but it no longer touches the benefits of current retirees:
\begin{align}\label{\refeq:esc:leadFOC}
    \dfrac{d\upsilon_{2,t}^i}{d\theta_{t+1}} &= (1-\alpha)\dfrac{d\ln\left(\Theta_{h,t}\right)}{d\theta_{t+1}}, &
    \dfrac{d\upsilon_{1,t}^i}{d\theta_{t+1}} &= (1+\beta_{t,i})\dfrac{d\ln\left(\tilde{c}_{1,t}^i\right)}{d\theta_{t+1}} + \beta_{t,i}\dfrac{d\ln\left(R_{t+1}\right)}{d\theta_{t+1}},
\end{align}
where each total derivative includes the response of the continuation policy $\tau_{t+1}$, and of $\theta_{t+2}$, to the design being chosen --- the former now also through the cost, since $f$ depends on $\tau_{t+1}$. Retirees are unanimous here --- the first expression does not depend on $i$ --- and favour a more contributive system, since a higher $\theta_{t+1}$ raises the return to an hour worked at $t$ and hence $h_t$, on which their benefits are levied. The young are divided, and the sign of the aggregate is a quantitative matter.

\smalltitle{Permanent.} At a reform date $t_0$ the electorate chooses a design expected to hold forever, $\theta_t = \theta$ for all $t\geq t_0$. Before $t_0$ the design is exogenous and constant at its calibrated value, and the vote is \emph{anticipated}: households arrive at $t_0$ knowing a design will be chosen, and what the electorate selects among is which of the equilibrium paths indexed by $\theta$ the economy follows from $t_0$ on. This is not an unanticipated reform, and the difference is not cosmetic --- it is what determines the value of the predetermined savings ratio the vote is taken against, as \cref{\refnumsec:ESC} sets out. All three channels operate at once: the sequential re-split of \eqref{\refeq:esc:seqFOC}, the labour-supply channel of \eqref{\refeq:esc:leadFOC}, and the continuation, which now moves the entire future path of taxes rather than one period of it. The marginal effects add,
\begin{align}\label{\refeq:esc:permFOC}
    \dfrac{d\upsilon_{2,t_0}^i}{d\theta} &= (1-\alpha)\dfrac{d\ln\left(\Theta_{h,t_0}\right)}{d\theta} + \mathcal{E}_{2,t_0}^{i,\theta}, &
    \dfrac{d\upsilon_{1,t_0}^i}{d\theta} &= (1+\beta_{t_0,i})\dfrac{d\ln\left(\tilde{c}_{1,t_0}^i\right)}{d\theta} + \beta_{t_0,i}\dfrac{d\ln\left(R_{t_0+1}\right)}{d\theta},
\end{align}
with $\mathcal{E}_{2,t_0}^{i,\theta}$ the summand of \eqref{\refeq:esc:seqFOC}. The electorate chooses $\tau_{t_0}$ and $\theta$ together, which appears to make the problem two-dimensional. It does not:

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:esc:concentration}
Under the permanent timing, the optimal tax at any candidate design is the ordinary politico-economic equilibrium tax at that design, $\tau^{\ast}(\theta) = \mathcal{T}_{t_0}(\theta)$, and the joint problem concentrates to a one-dimensional maximisation over $\theta$.
\end{proposition}

The argument is immediate once stated: $\partial\mathcal{W}_{t_0}/\partial\tau_{t_0} = 0$, taken with $s_{t_0-1,i}/s_{t_0-1}$ held fixed, \emph{is} the first order condition $z_{t_0}=0$ evaluated at $\theta_{t_0}=\theta$ --- including the factor $\left(1+\tau_{t_0}\partial_{\tau}\ln f\right)$ of \eqref{\refeq:esc:dlnc2i}, which $z_{t_0}$ carries. The permanent timing adds nothing to it, because $\theta$ is not a function of $\tau$. Likewise, for $t>t_0$ the design is fixed and the continuation is the ordinary equilibrium of \cref{\refsec:PEELOG} at a constant $\theta$ --- there is no recursion in the design to solve.


\subsubsection{Two structural properties of the log case}\label{\refsec:ESC:separability}
The log specification buys more than tractability here; it removes state variables from the design problem altogether.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:esc:separability}
Under log preferences, the date-$t$ political objective is additively separable,
\begin{align}\label{\refeq:esc:separability}
    \mathcal{W}_t = \mathcal{A}\left(\tau_t,\theta_t\right) + \mathcal{B}\left(\theta_{t+1}\right) + \mathcal{C}\left(s_{t-1}\right),
\end{align}
where $\mathcal{C}$ depends on no policy. Consequently the leaded choice of $\theta_{t+1}$ depends on neither $s_{t-1}$ nor $\theta_t$: the maximiser of \eqref{\refeq:esc:separability} is the maximiser of $\mathcal{B}$ alone.
\end{proposition}

To see \eqref{\refeq:esc:separability}, note that $\Theta_{h,t}$ in \eqref{\refeq:esc:auxiliary:Thetah} is a product of a factor in $\tau_t$ and a factor in $\left(\tau_{t+1},\theta_{t+1}\right)$, so $\ln h_t$ is a sum of a term in $\tau_t$, a term in $\theta_{t+1}$, and $\frac{\alpha\xi}{1+\alpha\xi}\ln(s_{t-1}/\nu_t)$. The same then holds for $\ln \tilde c_{1,t}^i = \frac{1+\xi}{\xi}\ln h_t + \ln\left[\cdot\right]$, whose bracket involves $\left(\theta_{t+1},\tau_{t+1}\right)$ only --- and $\tau_{t+1} = \mathcal{T}_{t+1}(\theta_{t+1})$ by decoupling, so $\theta_{t+1}$ only --- and for $\ln R_{t+1}$, which is affine in $\ln s_t$ and $\ln h_{t+1}$ and hence again in $\ln \Theta_{h,t}$ plus objects dated $t+1$ and later. Finally $\ln c_{2,t}^i = (1-\alpha)\ln h_t + \ln\left[s_{t-1,i}/s_{t-1} + \frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\tau_t\left(A(\theta_t,\tau_t)y^{\eta}_{t-1,i}/\Gamma_{h,t-1} + B(\theta_t,\tau_t)\right)\right] + \alpha\ln(s_{t-1}/\nu_t) + \text{const}$, whose second term involves $\left(\tau_t,\theta_t\right)$ and not $\theta_{t+1}$. Every dependence on $\theta_{t+1}$ therefore arrives through $\ln h_t$, additively.

\Cref{prop:esc:separability} is worth emphasising because it contradicts the natural reading of the leaded timing. One would expect $\theta_t$ to be a state variable at $t$, since it is chosen a period in advance and inherited; and it \emph{is} a state for the tax, through $z_t\left(\tau_t,\theta_t\right)$. It is not a state for the design: the electorate's preferred $\theta_{t+1}$ is the same whatever system it inherits. The property is specific to log preferences --- under CRRA the powers do not separate and both $s_{t-1}$ and $\theta_t$ re-enter --- and it is verified numerically rather than relied upon, in \cref{\refnumsec:ESC}.

\Cref{prop:esc:separability} does not extend to the permanent timing, where $\theta$ appears in $\mathcal{A}$ and $\mathcal{B}$ at once. There the predetermined ratio $s_{t_0-1,i}/s_{t_0-1}$ requires care on two counts. It is a function of the design in force at $t_0$, and yet the savings behind it are sunk when the vote is taken, so the electorate takes it as given: holding it fixed while varying the candidate is not an approximation but the definition of the problem. And because the vote is anticipated, the level at which it is held is the one the winning design implies, which makes the permanent equilibrium a fixed point rather than a single maximisation. \Cref{\refnumsec:ESC} gives the algorithm and reports what is at stake in getting either half wrong.
