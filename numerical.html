D2. Numerical checks

Section 4 of the paper computes date-specific Markov policy functions by backward induction along the demographic transition, and tests every candidate equilibrium at each state; the technical documentation (Berg and Gonzalez-Eiras 2026) gives the algorithms. The first table compares the date-specific solution with a time-invariant policy function, the approximation of much of the quantitative politico-economic literature, at the same dates. The second, appendix D’s, counts by the solves that wrote them, and the third for every file of results that records them, the equilibria and the candidates found at any state, and the states at which no candidate passed the test. There the solver falls back on a rule of the technical documentation: it ranks the candidate taxes by the integral of the first order condition, and the designs by the political objective with each design at its own consistent distribution of savings. The tables record a fallback only in the choice of the design. A single equilibrium at every state means that the selection rule of section 4 never had to choose.

The horizon and the terminal condition

Appendix D of the paper solves the calibrated economies again with the backward induction started two periods earlier, three periods later and under its steady-state terminal condition, and reports the outcomes in the calibration year and one period later. The table gives the change against the paper’s horizon at every date to the last dated period. Under log preferences the U.S. outcomes do not depend on the horizon. Elsewhere a longer horizon or the steady-state terminal condition moves the tax rate by a few hundredths of a percentage point at most at any date, and the chosen design by less than a thousandth. A horizon two periods shorter moves the outcomes of the calibration year as little, but those of the last dated periods by more, since its terminal period is then close. At the paper’s horizon every date it reports is within a few hundredths of a percentage point of its value under the steady-state terminal condition.
