\subsection{Calibration}\label{\refsec:calibration}
The calibration is a nested fixed point. In the inner part we solve the full politico-economic equilibrium path of \cref{\refsec:PEEpath}; in the outer part we search over the four parameters that cannot be set without it. The target system of \cref{\refmodelsec:calibration} is
\begin{subequations}\label{\refeq:calibration}
    \begin{align}
        \widehat{K/Y}_{t_0} &= n_{\text{yr}}\frac{s_{t_0-1}/\nu_{t_0}}{\left(s_{t_0-1}/\nu_{t_0}\right)^{\alpha} h_{t_0}^{1-\alpha}} = n_{\text{yr}}\frac{\alpha}{R_{t_0}} \label{\refeq:calibration:KY}\\
        \hat{\tau}_{t_0} &= \tau_{t_0} \label{\refeq:calibration:tau}\\
        \hat{\eta}_{0} &= \frac{z_0^{\eta}}{z_0^x}\frac{(1-\alpha)(1-\tau_{t_0})}{M_{t_0}\Theta_{h,t_0}^{\alpha}\left(\frac{1-\alpha}{\Gamma_{h,t_0}^{\alpha}}\right)^{\frac{1}{1+\alpha\xi}}} \label{\refeq:calibration:eta0}\\
        \hat{X}_{0} &= \hat{\eta}_{0}\frac{\left(\frac{1-\alpha}{\Gamma_{h,t_0}^{\alpha}}\right)^{\frac{1}{1+\alpha\xi}}}{\left(\Theta_{h,t_0}z_0^xM_{t_0}\right)^{1/\xi}} \label{\refeq:calibration:X0}
    \end{align}
\end{subequations}
where the hats denote the targets (for $\eta_0$ and $X_0$, the values implied by the solved equilibrium, which must reproduce the trial ones) and $t_0$ the baseline year. In our main specification $\beta_{t,j} = \beta p_{t,j}$ with a scalar $\beta$, and most exogenous parameters are constant over time except for the ageing population reflected by $\nu_t$. Loosely, $\beta$ carries the capital--output ratio, $\omega$ the tax rate, and $\eta_0, X_0$ the informal income and hours targets; that assignment describes the dominant terms of the Jacobian only.

The factor $n_{\text{yr}}=30$ in \eqref{\refeq:calibration:KY} is the number of years in a model period: $Y_{t_0}$ is thirty years of production while $s_{t_0-1}/\nu_{t_0}$ is a stock. The savings rate
\begin{align}
    \text{sr}_{t_0} &= \frac{s_{t_0}}{\left(s_{t_0-1}/\nu_{t_0}\right)^{\alpha} h_{t_0}^{1-\alpha}} \label{\refeq:calibration:sr}
\end{align}
is reported but not targeted, since with full depreciation it is $K_{t_0+1}/Y_{t_0}$ rather than an annual saving rate (\cref{\refmodelsec:calibration}). The capital--output ratio needs no such conversion, is the moment the developed-economy model identifies through its thirty-year interest rate, and depends on the predetermined stock $s_{t_0-1}$ alone.

Everything else is set before the loop: $\bm{\eta}_i,\bm{X}_i$ from \eqref{\refmodeleq:calibration:etai}--\eqref{\refmodeleq:calibration:Xi}, or \eqref{\refmodeleq:calibration:etaCommonX} under common $X$, $\theta$ from the replacement-rate ratio, and the pre-reform $\epsilon$ from the basic pension. Since $\epsilon$ depends on $\beta$, and $\kappa$ on $\epsilon$, these two are refreshed inside the loop.

\smalltitle{Two parameters that are not calibrated.}
The capital--output ratio \eqref{\refeq:calibration:KY} measures the \emph{formal} sector only: the informal households' savings $\iota_{t_0}s_{t_0}$ stay out of the capital stock by construction, and the denominator excludes informal output $w_{t_0}^0\eta_{t_0,0}h_{t_0,0}$ per informal household correspondingly, so $\iota$ is left free as a diagnostic; a national-accounts reading would make $\beta$ answerable to the informal block as well. The informal return scaler $\chi_t^R$ is fixed at one outside the loop, alongside $\xi$ and $\rho$, because nothing in the data we use identifies it. It is not a neutral default: $\chi^R = 1$ is the knife-edge at which the informal vehicle differs from formal saving only through the pension system, and $\chi^R$ enters both $B_{t+1}^0$ in \eqref{\refeq:auxiliary:B0} and $A_t$ in \eqref{\refeq:auxiliary:Ainf}, hence the level and the political weight of the informal savings ratio.

\smalltitle{Why $\eta_0$ and $X_0$ are the awkward pair.}
The informal productivity and disutility parameters set the informal households' consumption levels, and hence the weight of the informal terms of the first order condition; and the first term of $\iota_t$ in \eqref{\refeq:auxiliary:s0_s} is proportional to $\eta_{t,0}^{1+\xi}/X_{t,0}^{\xi}$, so they also set the scale of the political state. A trial $(\eta_0,X_0)$ therefore shifts the region of the state space in which the equilibrium lives. Two consequences follow. The state grids are rebuilt at every residual evaluation, since the bounds of $\mathcal{S}_0$ come from $\iota^*(\tau)$, which moves with $(\beta,\eta_0,X_0)$, and those of $\mathcal{S}$ from $s^*(\tau)$, which moves with $\beta$; a grid reused across the search would place the equilibrium outside its own grid, and the failure would show up as an infeasibility or a corner rather than as an error. And the Jacobian is not close to diagonal: $\eta_0$ and $X_0$ move $\tau_{t_0}$ through the state, and $\Theta_{h,t_0}$ appears in both \eqref{\refeq:calibration:eta0} and \eqref{\refeq:calibration:X0}, so the four equations are solved jointly.

\smalltitle{The outer residual is only piecewise smooth.}
The composed map from parameters to targets passes through a grid search. Within a branch it is continuous, since the selection rule locates $\tau_t$ by linear interpolation \emph{inside} a cell of $\mathcal{T}$ rather than snapping to a node, but its slope changes where a crossing passes a node; and it can jump where the selection of \cref{\refsec:RobustRoot} switches between well-separated candidates, or where a feasibility mask gains or loses a node. Three design choices follow.

\emph{The finite-difference step for the outer Jacobian is the default one}, $\sqrt{\text{machine precision}}$, in both preference cases: a small step stays on one smooth piece and returns its slope, which is what a Newton step wants, whereas a large step averages across kinks. The precondition is that nothing inside the residual carries a hidden discrete choice at that scale, which the fixed-knot smoothing of \cref{\refalg:LOG:gridsearch} guarantees.

\emph{The continuation interpolants are cubic, in both solvers.} With piecewise-linear continuation surfaces the composed map is kinked at every cell boundary of the state grid and a Newton iteration on the outer problem stalls at the kink scale: the CRRA calibration does not converge away from $\rho = 1$, and the log solution does not converge as $\mathcal{S}_0$ is refined. Both solvers use the same interpolant and the same fixed-knot smoother, because the calibration at $\rho = 1$ seeds the march across $\rho$ below. A plain cubic can overshoot where a policy is flat at a bound; a monotone (PCHIP) interpolant would rule that out, at a much higher cost per evaluation in two dimensions.

\emph{Grid convergence is verified by refinement.} A small residual can be present at a displaced answer, because the residual is computed \emph{through} the inner grids. The check, recorded with every calibrated point, refines the inner state grids at the calibrated parameters and re-evaluates: a converged answer's residual decays, a displaced one's plateaus. On this criterion the CRRA calibration runs its inner grids at $\vert\mathcal{S}\vert=\vert\mathcal{S}_0\vert=45$, finer than the politico-economic solve alone requires, while the log case is grid-converged at its default resolution.

An inner solve that fails (a path leaving the reachable set \eqref{\refeq:reachable}, or the initial fixed point \eqref{\refeq:initialFixedPoint} without a sign change) propagates the failure rather than returning a large sentinel residual, which would invite the root finder into a region where every evaluation fails alike; a failed calibration restores the parameters in place when it started.

\begin{alg}[Nested fixed point calibration]\label{\refalg:calibration}
Fix $\xi,\rho,\chi^R$ and the targets $\widehat{K/Y}_{t_0},\hat{\tau}_{t_0},z_0^{\eta},z_0^x$. Set $\bm{\eta}_i,\bm{X}_i$ and $\theta$ once, outside the loop.

\smallskip\noindent\emph{Outer step.} Search over $\left(\beta,\omega,\eta_0,X_0\right)$, each mapped to the real line by a logarithm so that positivity is enforced without a bound being reached. $\beta$ is \emph{not} capped at unity: a cap makes the search stall on the bound rather than fail informatively.

\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inner step.} Given a trial $\left(\beta,\omega,\eta_0,X_0\right)$:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Write the parameters into the parameter set and refresh the auxiliary definitions that depend on them, in order: $\epsilon$ from $\beta$, then $\kappa$ from $\epsilon$, then $\Gamma_h$ from $\left(\bm{\eta},\bm{X}\right)$.
    \item Rebuild the state grids at the trial parameters, $\mathcal{S}_0$ from $\iota^*(\tau)$ and, in the CRRA case, $\mathcal{S}$ from $s^*(\tau)$, at $\vert\mathcal{S}\vert=\vert\mathcal{S}_0\vert=45$ in the CRRA case, with cubic continuation interpolants in both.
    \item Solve the politico-economic equilibrium: \cref{\refalg:LOG:gridsearch} or \cref{\refalg:CRRA:grid} for the policy functions, then \cref{\refsec:PEEpath} for the initial state, the forward simulation and the exact re-solve of the economic equilibrium at the simulated $\bm{\tau}$.
    \item Read off the four quantities in \eqref{\refeq:calibration} at $t_0$, recovering $\Theta_{h,t_0}$ from the solved $\left(h_{t_0},s_{t_0-1}\right)$ by inverting \eqref{\refeq:auxiliary:h}, which covers both preference cases and the general and terminal formulas with one expression.
\end{enumerate}

\smallskip\noindent\emph{Residual.} Form the gap for \eqref{\refeq:calibration:tau} as a level difference and the other three as relative differences. The capital--output ratio is an order of magnitude larger than the tax rate, so a level gap on \eqref{\refeq:calibration:KY} would let the root finder trade accuracy in the tax rate for accuracy in the capital--output ratio; for \eqref{\refeq:calibration:eta0} and \eqref{\refeq:calibration:X0} a relative gap is the natural form for a self-consistency condition.

\smallskip\noindent\emph{Common $X$.} The root is solved at $X = 1$. $X$ then follows from \eqref{\refmodeleq:calibration:Xsolve} at the solved $h_{t_0}$, $\eta_0$ and $X_0$ are re-derived at the unchanged $\Theta_{h,t_0}$ and $\tau_{t_0}$, and the equilibrium is re-solved to confirm that the targets are unchanged.
\end{alg}

\smalltitle{Calibrating across a grid of $\rho$.}
The calibrations across $\rho$ are organised as a march anchored at $\rho=1$, the only value at which the cheaper log solver applies. The anchor is started from a guess near its solution, since at default parameters the steady-state informal ratio can fall outside the net-saver region, leaving the state grid degenerate. The march then walks outwards in both directions and starts each calibration from an extrapolation through the two solved points nearest it, taken in the \emph{unbounded} coordinate of the outer search, since extrapolating a positivity-constrained parameter in levels can step across its bound. A point that fails is retried from the previous solution and then through an intermediate value inserted before it. Agreement between the log calibration and a CRRA calibration at $\rho$ close to $1$ tests both solvers, which share no code path. Across $\rho$ the calibrated $\eta_0$ and $X_0$ barely move while $\beta$ and $\omega$ move substantially: the self-consistency conditions \eqref{\refeq:calibration:eta0}--\eqref{\refeq:calibration:X0} are very nearly solved by direct substitution, and it is $\left(\beta,\omega\right)$ that the data targets identify.
